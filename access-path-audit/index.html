The method

The Access-Path Audit

A result can be real and still not show what the claim says. The audit follows a claim back along the path that produced it, and returns the strongest claim the evidence actually supports.

The Institute's founder has built diagnostic AI himself, for stroke rehabilitation and fall risk, and knows where such claims fail (Roots). The audit applies to an experiment, a published paper, a diagnostic algorithm, or a start-up's central claim. It is Thesis 11 of the programme, argued in Chapter 12. Auditing a claim along the path it travelled.

  1. Step 1

    Target

    What relation does the claim assert: infection, cause, diagnosis, adaptation?

    Claim 11.1

  2. Step 2

    Route

    Which instruments, samples and decisions stand between the target and the result?

    Claim 11.5

  3. Step 3

    Product and rival route

    What did the chain actually stabilise, and could a named artifact have produced the same result? Replication counts only if it changes that dependency.

    Claim 11.3

  4. Step 4

    The gap

    Where does the claim say more than the product shows? That step is where overinterpretation enters.

    Claim 11.1

  5. Step 5

    Residual claim

    What is the strongest claim the evidence licenses once the rival routes are excluded? Evidential status does not travel automatically.

    Claim 11.2

Worked cases

Where claims outran their access

Virology

XMRV and chronic fatigue syndrome

A retrovirus was reported in patients' blood and then found again by further tests. The repeated positives shared contamination routes through reagents and cell lines, so replication did not change the dependency that mattered. The audit reconstructs where the claim outran its access.

Claim 11.9

Medical AI

AI that read the hospital, not the lungs

Deep-learning models reported high accuracy for COVID-19 on chest radiographs. Tested outside their source datasets, several relied on shortcuts such as image markers and dataset origin rather than pathology. The output was real; the claim of detecting disease was not supported.

DeGrave, Janizek & Lee (2021). AI for radiographic COVID-19 detection selects shortcuts over signal. Nature Machine Intelligence. doi:10.1038/s42256-021-00338-7

Imaging

What a microscope image can carry

A fluorescence image is a reproducible product of labels, optics and processing. The audit records which part of a biological claim depends on each step, and which rival route could produce the same picture.

Claim 11.7

Cosmology

Four claims under one name

'The Big Bang' names several claims with different access paths and different warrant. Separating them shows which parts rest on measurement and which on model inference.

The claims behind the method

Thesis 11 in full

The thesis

Error in empirical science enters at the step from what an access chain stabilized to the target relation a claim asserts. A claim-level audit locates that step, tests robustness against named artifact routes, and outputs the strongest residual claim the evidence licenses.

    Non-automatic claim-status inheritance and residual claims Central thesis

    Warrant does not pass automatically from a result to a transformed claim. Content reused for a different target, relation or strength needs its warrant re-established, and the audit outputs the strongest residual claim that survives.
