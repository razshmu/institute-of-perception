הטענות

הטענות מוצגות בנוסח ההפקדה, באנגלית. זה הנוסח המחייב.

  • 13.1
    Target Priority Principle and the typed target-to-claim graph Central thesis

    Claim-bearing data do not precede target individuation: a scientific claim is the terminal node of a typed dependency graph in which every load-bearing edge carries a target-continuity status (preserved, transformed with bridge, replaced,…

  • 13.2
    Error inheritance: downstream correctness cannot repair upstream misindividuation Specific extension

    If an early assignment maps two world states that differ in the claim-relevant target to the same labelled data, every downstream stage without independent target evidence yields identical output distributions, so no downstream analysis…

  • 13.3
    Cross-target validation as a third level of objectivity Specific extension

    When target identity is disputed, objectivity requires cross-target validation, testing whether a claim survives live alternative target partitions or receiver models, in addition to within-route and cross-route validation.

  • 13.4
    Target-space conservatism hypothesis Testable hypothesis

    The capacity to register relations grows faster than the capacity to formulate targets, so early uses of a new instrument deepen established target classes, and major conceptual advances coincide with target reconstruction.

  • 13.5
    Perceptual pointability predicts definitional disputes in science Testable hypothesis

    Scientific disputes over what counts as one instance concentrate on concepts whose referents human perception does not deliver as bounded, cohesive and persisting objects and that no instrument has converted into such objects.

  • 13.6
    Relation-retention profile of a narrowing step Operationalisation

    Each load-bearing transition in a scientific route is described by a vector, not a scalar: claim-relative risk reduction G(C), foreclosure L(A) for each live alternative, relation-space contraction K, reversibility V, and target status.
