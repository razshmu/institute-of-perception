Institute of Perception

Perception comes first.

A bee sees this tulip differently from you. That difference is not a detail. What an animal can sense decides what it eats, whom it mates with, and how it evolves. What an instrument can measure decides what science can claim.

The Institute of Perception studies both, and trains young researchers to do the same.

From the founder

I founded the Institute of Perception around one observation. Science usually treats the receiver as neutral: the eye, the sensor, the instrument, the observer. It is not neutral. It decides what gets in.

In animals, that decision can steer evolution. In science, it decides what a result can honestly claim. We study both, we publish every claim with the test that could refute it, and we bring young researchers into the work early.

Shmuel Raz, PhDFounder and Director

The method

The Access-Path Audit

In 2020, AI models "detected COVID" on chest X-rays. Several had learned which hospital an image came from, not whether the patient was ill. A result can be real and still not show what the claim says.

The audit follows a claim back along its path: what the claim is about, how it was measured, what the measurement produced, and what else could have produced the same result. It ends with the strongest claim the evidence actually supports. It works on an experiment, a paper, a diagnostic algorithm, or a start-up's central claim.

Why the receiver matters

Same light, different windows

A honeybee sees ultraviolet and misses red. Many birds see ultraviolet too. Each receiver cuts a different window into the same light, and selection acts on what passes through.

Young Minds

Real research, earlier than usual

The Institute's mission is to teach young people to ask what the evidence really shows. Selected students aged 11–17 do genuine research with the founder, and learn the Institute's method on their own questions.
