What would refute this, and what to run first

The question

Sixteen chapters have introduced sixteen theses, each with at least one falsifier, because no entry is admitted without one.

This chapter does not restate them. It sorts them. Which failures would cost a single entry, and which would cost a whole part? Of the tests available, which are both decisive and affordable? And what is the programme careful not to claim, so that a sympathetic reader does not credit it with more than it asks for?

Failures that would cost a part

Most of these falsifiers are local. A handful are structural, in that a single result would remove a part of the argument rather than one entry.

Part I rests on non-modularity. Its engine is that one perceptual change reaches many behaviours. Show that perceptual changes are modular, confined to their own signal class as Chapter 2 sets out, and three things fall at once. Receiver-first routes lose their motive force, the amplification argument in Chapter 11 loses its premise, and the loop in Chapter 3 has nothing to start it.

Part I also rests on its diagnostics. Chapter 3’s contrasts claim to separate causal routes. If they do not, in simulation or in experiment, the part holds a hypothesis with no method attached. The simulation reported there is the first instalment of that test, and it does not pass: classification from observation alone ran at 44%.

Part II rests on one proved result. Chapter 13’s access map — that no processing can split what an access procedure could not distinguish — carries the arguments about alignment, about inheritance of error, and about narrowing. Its application fails if a purely computational step, with no new coupling, splits such a class.

Part II’s account of objectivity rests on independence doing work. Chapter 15 stakes it on an empirical comparison, and that comparison could come out the wrong way.

Part III rests on rescaling being real. Chapter 16’s normalisation must separate speed from shape. If shape differences survive it, or each context needs its own fitted timescale, the coordinate is a curve-fitting device. Chapter 17’s reconciliation fails differently: by a case that satisfies the impossibility conditions and is nonetheless covered by the construction, without delay.

Three tests worth running first

What would decide a question and what a laboratory can afford are two different things. Three score on both, and each is specified in its chapter rather than here.

Test Chapter Decides Cost
Gene edit, cue field held 2 whether a receiver change alone starts divergence one edited line, one season
Split-brood tutoring 9 whether a category boundary is heritable, and separates calibration from learning rule one breeding design
Stage one of the alignment test 13 whether an alignment score is stable enough to attribute at all no retraining

Two of the three are cheap. All three are decisive against a named claim rather than suggestive about a general one.

That ordering reflects a finding rather than a preference. Chapter 11 ended on a blocked test and the reason it was blocked: the compounds with measured genetic covariance and the compounds with measured perceptual grouping do not overlap at a single molecule. The limiting resource is measurements of what animals discriminate — not genetic data, which are plentiful, and not statistical technique.

Four things the programme does not claim

Each is stated plainly. Each is easy to over-read in the opposite direction.

Not a metaphysics. Chapter 15 gave the three-way distinction between a method, a research programme and a metaphysical proposal. The programme takes the first two positions and declines the third.

Not an argument against a world independent of receivers. Bounded invariance competes with the no-miracles argument as an explanation of success. It does not refute realism, and Chapter 15 says why no experiment could.

Not a claim about how often. Chapter 2 treats frequency as an open empirical question with stated conditions for abandoning a receiver-first reading in any given system.

Not a dismissal of confounded measurement. Systems sharing a route can still disagree, and their agreement remains informative. What is denied is that the number assigns the agreement to a source.

How this ends

Sixteen theses, three registers, one recurring move. Something normally left in the background is brought into the causal account. A quantity that looked like a property of the world then turns out to be a property of a relation, and that relation can be measured, varied, and in some cases selected on.

Whether that is right is not settled here. This volume introduces; the entries commit; the tests decide. The guarantee throughout is that a reader who disagrees can find the exact entry to attack and the exact result that would end the matter.

    Objectivity as delta-robust invariance across independent access procedures Central thesis
