Every system has its own clock

The question

Time looks like the one thing that is not receiver-relative. A second is a second for a fly and for a galaxy.

Yet a duration is always counted against some process — a caesium transition, an orbit, a heartbeat, a neural integration window. The question is what follows when systems whose processes run at widely different rates are compared, and whether the comparison is doing something that goes undeclared.

What the thesis says

The opening claim is procedural and disarming. Any comparison between systems requires a declared normalisation frame, and when none is declared, the frame defaults silently to the observer’s own system. The year is the standing example: cosmic history is narrated in units of one planet’s orbit around one star.

This is not an objection to using years. It is an objection to not saying so, because an undeclared frame cannot be varied, and a frame that cannot be varied cannot be checked.

The constructive proposal is a time coordinate built from the system’s own characteristic timescale: an effective time obtained by integrating against that scale. It comes with a criterion that makes it testable rather than merely available. If a context changes only the rate, trajectories should coincide in the rescaled coordinate. Any residual difference in shape therefore marks changed dynamics rather than changed speed.

That is a real discriminator: speed and shape come apart, and the normalisation separates them. Its falsifier is stated in the same breath — shape differences persisting after normalisation, or different contexts requiring different best-fitting timescales.

The biological half of the thesis makes temporal processing a trait. Integration windows are selectable, and their calibration differs by stimulus class, so that an organism’s perceptual field divides into slowly integrated stable segments and quickly integrated dynamic ones. The falsifier is comparative: windows uniform across stimulus classes within a species, or between-class differences tracking phylogeny alone once temperature and body size are controlled.

A further prediction holds that the rescaling does real work across species. Sensory temporal-resolution data should align better in the rescaled coordinate than in seconds, with the scale set by flicker-fusion frequency or by a metabolic mapping fitted on separate species. The falsifier is precise — no dispersion reduction beyond a single global rescaling, no gain on held-out species, or success only when the model is allowed a free parameter per species.

A worked example

The most striking application is not biological. It is the structure of galaxies.

A disk galaxy’s morphology and bar strength reach a saturated state after roughly ten to twenty internal orbital periods at a characteristic radius — and this is claimed to hold independently of the galaxy’s mass. In years, galaxies of different masses settle at different times. In orbits, they settle after the same count.

The same move is applied to halos. At fixed mass, halos that have lived more internal dynamical times since their last major merger should show lower scatter in concentration, with each halo’s clock set by the mean density inside its own scale radius. Systems that look heterogeneous when compared in one shared external time look orderly when each is compared against its own.

The falsifiers are quantitative. For disks: bar strength, disk thickness or bulge-to-disk ratio continuing to trend beyond twenty orbits in matched samples. For halos: concentration scatter at fixed mass not falling with internal age, or internal age adding no predictive power beyond formation redshift.

The whole approach has a stated point of failure. The hierarchical framing depends on a separation of timescales between a system and its host. Where that separation breaks down, a smooth time-flow description loses its power and dynamics appear punctuated and multi-scale. The falsifier is a system with no scale separation that remains well described by one smooth timescale.

What would refute it

Gathered from above: shape differences surviving normalisation; uniform integration windows within a species; no dispersion reduction across species beyond a global rescaling. Add to those a disk that keeps changing past twenty orbits, halo scatter indifferent to internal age, and a system without scale separation that a single timescale still describes.

One further claim is testable and, on its face, unlikely: cycles at adjacent levels — atomic, biological, planetary, galactic — should lock at commensurate ratios more often than chance. The falsifier is a null result against a random model, which is the right test and an easy one to run.

The floor and the sky

The thesis ends with a claim that states the general form of everything Part III is about.

Every system registers processes much faster and smaller than its own reference scale as stable matter, and processes much slower and larger as a fixed sky. What appears solid is what moves too fast to follow; what appears permanent is what moves too slowly to notice.

This is the temporal version of the perceptual niche from Chapter 2. A receiver’s rate does not only determine how much detail it resolves. It determines which parts of the world appear as objects and which appear as background — and the two categories are assigned by the relation between the system’s rate and the world’s, not by the world alone.

The claim is checkable. Measurement error should grow with the gap between rates, and the effect should collapse under scale-matched clocks. If neither happens, the floor and the sky are not artefacts of position.

The claims this chapter rests on

  • 14.1
    Declared normalization frame for cross-system comparison Central thesis

    Any comparison between systems requires a declared normalization frame; when none is declared, the frame defaults silently to the observer’s own system, as the Earth-orbit year does for cosmic history.

  • 14.2
    Class-specific integration windows (Unified Perceptual Field) Specific extension

    Temporal integration windows are selectable traits, and their calibration by stimulus class splits an organism’s perceptual field into long-integration stable segments and short-integration dynamic segments.

  • 14.9
    Disk galaxies saturate after a fixed number of orbits Testable hypothesis

    Disk galaxy morphology and bar strength reach a stable, saturated state after about 10–20 internal orbital periods at a characteristic radius, independent of galaxy mass.

  • 14.11
    Flow continuity fails when scale separation fails Testable hypothesis

    Where ε_x ≪ 1 is violated, a smooth time-flow description loses explanatory power and dynamics appear punctuated and multi-scale.

  • 14.12
    Floor and sky: scale position fixes matter and space Testable hypothesis

    Each system registers processes much faster and smaller than its own reference scale as stable matter and processes much slower and larger as a fixed sky.

  • 14.14
    Constructed time Θ and the speed-versus-shape criterion Operationalisation

    An organism’s effective time coordinate is Θ(t) = ∫dt/τ(t); if a context changes only τ, behavioural trajectories coincide in Θ, so any residual shape difference marks changed dynamics rather than changed speed.
