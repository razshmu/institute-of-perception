Auditing a claim along the path it travelled

The question

Part I argued that what an organism can know about its world is shaped by the receivers it has. Part II turns the argument on the enterprise making that argument.

A scientific result does not arrive from the world. It arrives through a chain: an instrument, a sample, a reagent, a pipeline, a statistical model, a sentence. Each link stabilises something. The question is whether what the chain stabilised is the thing the claim is about — and, if not, how anyone would find out.

What the thesis says

The central observation is precise and uncomfortable. A reproducible access product can stabilise an operational relation that differs from the target relation its claim asserts. The result replicates; the instrument is reliable; the statistics are sound. And the claim is still about something other than what was measured.

Where does this enter? Not at the statistics, not at the risk-of-bias assessment, not in the reporting — those are all downstream. It enters at the step from the operational relation to the target relation, which is exactly the step that standard checklists do not cover.

Three consequences follow.

Warrant does not travel by itself. When content is reused for a different target, a different relation or a stronger claim, its warrant must be re-established. The audit’s output is the strongest residual claim that survives the transformation, which may be considerably weaker than the one in the abstract.

Replication is not automatically evidence. Replication counts against a suspected artefact only when it changes, bounds or independently tests the dependency the artefact route runs through. Repeating a study along the same laboratory, reagent, cell line or pipeline reproduces the artefact faithfully. The falsifier is stated as a class of cases: if replication along a shared dependency exposed suspected artefacts as often as dependency-diverse replication did, the distinction would not matter.

The output is a verdict, not a score. The audit framework ends every case with a written corrected claim in one of four forms: stand, narrow, qualify, or refuse.

A worked example

The fully worked reconstruction is the episode linking the retrovirus XMRV to chronic fatigue syndrome.

What the audit preserves is the operational history: access chains did produce XMRV-like signals more often in patient samples than in controls. That is not disputed and not discarded.

What the audit refuses is the infection claim, and the reason is procedural rather than retrospective. Admissible contamination routes — mouse DNA in reagents, cell lines carrying related sequences — were never separated from the biological route. The signal was real as a signal. Its attribution to infection in patients was never licensed by the design.

The falsifier is stated in the form of a historical fact that would overturn the reading: if the record showed that positive studies used contamination-independent routes and still detected the virus, the refusal would be wrong.

A second example shows the framework applied to a name rather than a result. “The Big Bang” bundles four claims carrying four different grades of warrant. That the universe expands is observed. That it was hot and dense is a strong multi-route inference. That there was a singularity is a model extended past its domain of validity. That something came from nothing is not a claim of the theory at all. A dispute about “the Big Bang” is generally a dispute about which of the four is meant. The falsifier is direct: one observation or inference route supporting all four at the same grade.

What would refute it

The framework’s own pilot supplies the first test, and reports a result that could have gone against it. In a single-coder purposive pilot of thirteen claim instances from eleven sensory-ecology papers, six claims were preserved, five narrowed and two routed out of scope. The narrowings clustered at one transition: from sensory mechanism to behavioural or evolutionary conclusion.

That clustering is the finding, and the falsifier is aimed at it. Independent recoding that places the narrowings elsewhere, at the measurement of cues for instance, would show the framework reflecting a coder’s habits rather than a real weak point. A single coder is not enough.

Other components carry their own falsifiers. Against the replication claim, the class of cases described above. Against the audit of image-based profiling: the route-aware reading supports perturbation-associated image variation, makes phenotype identity conditional on batch separation, and supports a unique mechanism only where a molecular or perturbational route confirms it. Evidence that batch-confounded designs identify phenotypes as reliably as batch-separated ones would overturn the conditional.
