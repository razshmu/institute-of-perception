Science has an Umwelt too

The question

The previous chapter audited individual claims. This one asks about the enterprise: if every animal’s access to the world is shaped by its receivers, what shapes ours?

The question has a concrete test case. Artificial networks trained on different data, with different architectures, in different modalities, are reported to converge on similar internal representations — and this is read as evidence that they are converging on the structure of reality. It is the strongest contemporary claim that measurement can reach past the measurer. It is worth examining carefully.

What the thesis says

Human science is species-specific in its access conditions: sensory-cognitive, instrumental, representational and communal. Even access to formal validity is bodily, inscriptional and communal — a proof is checked by people reading marks. Objectivity is then achieved by discipline, not by escaping the conditions. The falsifier is stated as an impossible-sounding event, which is the point: a scientific result reaching the community with its content and warrant through no sensory, instrumental, notational or communal step.

A formal result underwrites the rest. Any access procedure defines a map from world states to access products. States the procedure cannot distinguish form equivalence classes, and no processing of the output can split those classes. Splitting a class requires a new coupling to the world. This is proved, not proposed, and its application has a stated failure condition: a purely computational step, with no new coupling, that distinguishes two states the original map sent to the same product.

From here the alignment case follows directly. A representational-alignment score depends on two generative sources — a shared target and a shared route — and on a measurement layer: the item set, the correspondence rule, the layer rule, the statistic. An association statistic computed over a chosen population of items cannot separate them.

One observation here is specific and awkward. Alignment clusters between artificial networks and brains are products of niche construction. People built the images, the categories and the benchmarks these systems train on, and those pass across model generations by ecological inheritance. Measured convergence may record a shared route that human beings constructed.

What would separate the explanations? Under substitution of the route, a shared target predicts that a named, calibrated relation survives every change, given preserved competence; an Umwelt source predicts selective change in one pre-named relation with a stable negative control. Different signatures, therefore separable — but only by substitution, not by a better score.

A worked example

The realist reading of the Platonic Representation Hypothesis rests its whole weight on one step.

The argument moves from the statistics of observations to the statistics of the world. That move is proved only for observation functions that are bijective and deterministic — lossless. Real observation is neither. And the scores that are reported as evidence are estimated on evaluation batches, which are not the training co-occurrence the theorem concerns.

The falsifier is exactly what a defender would need to supply: a demonstration that under realistic lossy observation the learned kernel still identifies world statistics, and that evaluation-batch scores consistently estimate the theoretical quantity.

The constructive half is a two-stage design. Stage one holds the trained systems fixed and varies only the measurement layer, with criteria fixed in advance; this establishes whether the measurement is adequate at all. Stage two varies data provenance, task constraint and inductive bias. Only the second stage can attribute, and it is worth running only if the first succeeds.

The argument is developed at length in a separate paper, Measured Convergence Does Not Identify Its Source. This chapter states the position; the paper carries the analysis.

What would refute it

Against the score claim: a general result showing that mutual-nearest-neighbour or CKA scores are invariant to item-set composition for systems that share a latent structure but allocate precision differently. If the measurement layer does not move the score, the confound is not there.

Against the access-map result: a computational step with no new coupling that splits an equivalence class.

Against the species-specificity claim: a result reaching the community through no sensory, instrumental, notational or communal step.

Against the decision-theoretic account of access: routes marked as ordered by the profile yielding decision performance ordered the opposite way in a benchmark.

Paradigms, read as sensory ecologies

One further claim in this thesis reframes a familiar idea. A scientific paradigm is described as a stabilised perceptual regime with three jointly necessary parts: calibration and access regimes, trained discrimination, and communal standards of validation. Paradigm change is then typed by which of the three moved.

This puts Kuhn’s notion into the same framework as Part I, without softening it into metaphor. Trained discrimination is a real skill acquired through practice; access regimes are instruments and protocols; communal standards are what a field will accept. A change in what scientists can see is a change in one of these, and the three come apart.

The claim has practical use rather than only interpretive interest. Asking which of the three moved is a sharper question than asking whether a paradigm shifted, and it can be answered from the historical record.
