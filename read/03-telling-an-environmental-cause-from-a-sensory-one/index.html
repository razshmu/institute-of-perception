Telling an environmental cause from a sensory one

The question

Suppose two populations differ, and the difference is real. The previous chapter offered one explanation: a receiver changed first. But the same field pattern is equally consistent with the environment having differed first, or with neither — with animals having sorted themselves into different places because of what they already preferred.

These are not shades of one story. They are different causal orders with different consequences, and in the field they look alike. The question is whether anything short of a complete reconstruction of history can tell them apart.

What the thesis says

The eco-sensory triangle is an answer in the form of a diagnostic grammar rather than a new theory. It reduces a cue-mediated system to three components on one causal graph: the external cue field, the receiver’s sensory state, and the interaction phenotype — what the animal does, where it goes, whom it meets.

The value of the reduction is that the competing theories become constrained subgraphs of the same graph. Sensory drive, receiver bias, matching habitat choice and niche construction stop being rival vocabularies and become different patterns of non-zero links. Once they are on one graph, the question “which theory is right here?” becomes “which links are active in this system?”, and that is a question interventions can answer.

The interventions are deliberately modest. Three contrasts do most of the work, and each can be run in separate tanks or matched arenas rather than reconstructed from field histories.

Hold the cue field flat. If the behavioural difference persists while the focal cue dimensions are held inside a declared tolerance band over the decision window, then a current-environment explanation is rejected for those dimensions. Not for all dimensions, and not for the past: the claim is bounded to the declared dimensions and the declared window.

Mask a channel, then restore it. Reversibly degrading one sensory channel, with sham and general-impairment controls, identifies whether that channel carries the difference.

What this does not show is the more important half. Masking establishes that a channel is involved in the behaviour now. It says nothing about the evolutionary route that produced the difference, and a positive result is compatible with every route in the list above.

These designs also separate four claims that are easy to run together, and that need different evidence. That a divergence is receiver-mediated at present is one claim. That it originated at the receiver is a second, and historical. That it is maintained by receiver differences is a third. That it feeds back into the field is a fourth. A flat-field result supports the first; none of the four follows from any other.

Turn feedback on and off. Feedback counts as receiver-initiated only when three links hold together: the receiver state predicts behaviour under a matched field, the behaviour durably modifies the field, and the modified field returns to affect later receiver state, behaviour or selection. One link is not enough, and the third — the return path — is the one that tends to be missing.

A fourth contrast addresses sorting directly. Randomising settlement, or equalising host, habitat, timing and encounter sequence, tests whether assortative mating was produced by non-random exposure: if it weakens while receiver state is unchanged, exposure was doing the work.

Systematically, holding each of the three components high, low or free defines twenty-seven regimes. Twelve of them — the ones with two components fixed and one free — are the clean local route tests. The grammar is finite, and most of it is not worth running.

A worked example

The clearest use of the triangle is negative, and it concerns a habit rather than a hypothesis.

Ecologists routinely characterise an animal’s environment by where the animal is actually found: realised habitat use, occupancy, encounter histories. The triangle shows why this is a trap. Where an organism is found is an outcome of its preference, its movement and its sensory state. Conditioning an analysis on realised use therefore manufactures associations between phenotype and environment through collider stratification — the association appears in the statistics without existing in the world.

This is not abstract. At Israel’s Evolution Canyons, slope membership for mobile organisms is itself a result of movement, settlement and retention. Trait-environment effects estimated from slope occupancy are therefore identified only with measured exposure fields or assigned exposure histories. The falsifier is exact: if effects estimated from occupancy turn out to equal those from assigned-exposure designs for mobile taxa there, the concern was misplaced.

Tried in simulation, the diagnostics return numbers that are not flattering to observation alone. Across two thousand simulated datasets spanning five route families, held-out classification of the true route ran at 44% from the observed pattern, rising to 64% once a flat-field contrast was added. That is the honest scale of the problem: watching does not identify the route, and a single cheap intervention buys a large improvement without solving it.

What would refute it

The thesis fails as a whole if the stated interventions do not separate the motifs, either in simulation or in experiment. That is the strongest form, and the simulation result above is the beginning of that test rather than its conclusion.

Individual components have their own falsifiers. If phenotype-environment associations estimated conditional on use turn out to equal those from assigned-exposure designs, the collider warning was unnecessary. If two regimes with similar endpoint matching cannot be separated by any intervention available in the model, the grammar does not do what it promises. If durable environmental divergence appears even with feedback switched off, the feedback account is wrong about what produced it.

One boundary deserves emphasis because it is easy to overstate in the animal’s favour. Sensory-driven adult host choice in plant-feeding insects counts as receiver-first sorting: it redirects encounters without modifying the cue field. Claiming that it modifies the field requires separate evidence about oviposition, larvae, microbes or substrate. Sorting and construction are different claims, and are kept apart throughout.

A derived quantity, not a fourth component

The triangle has three components, and a fourth does not belong. The experienced cue stream — the portion of the field an organism actually encounters and registers — is a derived readout at receiver scale, not a node. It is reported either as an exposure proxy built independently of receiver state, or as a receiver-space model that does not then count as evidence for receiver state.

The distinction is procedural rather than philosophical. If the experienced stream were treated as a component, it could be estimated from the receiver and then used to support conclusions about the receiver, which is circular. Keeping it derived blocks that circle.
