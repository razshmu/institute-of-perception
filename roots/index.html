Roots

How we came to the question

The Institute was founded in 2024, but its question is older. For twenty years Shmuel Raz worked on biodiversity, on developmental symmetry and on medical AI. Each line of work kept returning to the same problem: what a measurement actually captures, and who or what is doing the receiving.

This page shows earlier work by the Institute's founder, carried out at the University of Haifa, at Harvard and at CVAid Medical. It is background to the Institute, not Institute output.

1 · Biodiversity

Two worlds a few hundred metres apart

At Evolution Canyon on Mount Carmel, a sun-baked slope faces a cool, shaded one. Counting scorpions and snails on both slopes, and following mole rats and spiny mice that live between them, showed populations diverging across a few hundred metres. Two of these studies were published in PNAS.

The Institute's question began here: if the environment differs by a slope, what decides how each animal experiences it?

2 · Symmetry

Measuring stress with a ruler

Organisms under stress grow slightly less symmetrically. Measuring thousands of leaves, shells and fungal colonies on both slopes turned small asymmetries into a measure of developmental stress, and the methods review is widely used.

It taught a lesson the Institute now applies everywhere: a measurement is only as good as its account of what it captures, and what else could produce the same number.

  • Graham JH, Raz S, Hel-Or H, Nevo E (2010). Fluctuating asymmetry: methods, theory, and applications. Symmetry 2: 466–540. doi:10.3390/sym2020466

3 · Medicine

Building diagnostic AI from the inside

When someone close to him was diagnosed with Parkinson's disease, Shmuel Raz turned to a new question: could a camera and an algorithm notice changes in movement too small for the eye? That work led to machine-learning systems for stroke rehabilitation, fall risk and eye-movement disorders, to U.S. patents, and to CVAid Medical, the AI stroke-diagnosis company he founded.

Having built diagnostic AI, he knows where a claim that 'the model diagnoses' can outrun what the model actually measures. That knowledge is the core of the Access-Path Audit.

  • Eichler N, Raz S, Toledano-Shubi A, Livne D, Shimshoni I, Hel-Or H (2022). Automatic and efficient fall risk assessment based on machine learning. Sensors 22: 1557. doi:10.3390/s22041557
