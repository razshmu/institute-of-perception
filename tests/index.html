Refute us

What would refute it

Every claim carries an outcome that would tell against it. A falsifier contradicts the claim as stated. An outcome that counts against a claim weakens or narrows it without contradicting it.

The failures that would cost a whole line of work, and the three tests worth running first, are in Chapter 18. What would refute this, and what to run first.

Evolution · Senses that start evolution

Thesis 1. A sensory change can start a divergence

ClaimKindOutcome
1.1Falsifier

A heritable perceptual variant changes discrimination of its target signal class only, with no change in foraging, habitat use or mate choice under matched cue fields.

1.2Falsifier

Lines differing only in the edited receiver gene show no divergence in mate choice or habitat preference under matched cue fields, or behaviour diverges only after cue-field divergence.

1.3Counts against

A programme has no direct falsifier. Its standing rests on 1.1 and 2.2.

1.4Counts against

Receiver-first dynamics appear in systems where receiver differences are neither repeatable nor heritable, or where the behaviour has no ecological reach.

1.5Falsifier

Swapping the sensory system between populations does not transfer both the preference and the performance difference.

1.6Falsifier

Within polymorphic platyrrhine groups, trichromats and dichromats do not differ in choice of red-shifted fruit or in seed deposition; or fruit colour shows no association with primate vision after controlling for bird dispersers.

1.7Falsifier

Restoring contrast in a masked system does not restore assortative mating, or habitat-area management yields equal or larger recovery.

1.8Counts against

One condition met in a system removes receiver-first status for that system; across systems, the route is rare if most tested cases meet one.

1.9Counts against

A genetic change in one sensory feature (for example CFF) produces behavioural or fitness change no larger than a comparable single morphological change.

1.10Counts against

Os has low repeatability or heritability; initial Os fails to predict change in Oi under matched E; full-loop models do not outperform reduced ones in intact-loop blocks.

1.11Counts against

The receiver claim fails if general sensitivity change explains the difference, if receiver change does not alter generalisation, if categories do not transfer to new stimuli, if the effect vanishes after controls for learning, motivation and motor ability, or if action is unaffected.

1.12Counts against

Edited and control lines show no difference in mate choice, habitat preference or environmental modification under matched cue fields.

1.13Falsifier

E does not diverge when feedback is enabled, or E diverges equally when masking removes Os-driven behavioural differences.

1.14Falsifier

Variants that differ at a receptor locus show identical behavioural discrimination profiles over the habitat’s measured cue dimensions.

1.15Counts against

The usage fails if decline and ascent on stated axes cannot be measured independently of each other.

2.2Falsifier

2.3Falsifier

Two regimes with similar endpoint matching are not separated by any intervention in the model, or durable E divergence appears under feedback-off.

2.4Counts against

Applying the standard to published cases changes none of their classifications relative to the existing magic/pseudomagic categories.

2.5Counts against

Os(t) adds no out-of-sample prediction of Oi(t+1) beyond prior Oi and E_exp in a system claimed as receiver-led.

2.6Counts against

Divergence disappears under matched cue dimensions.

2.7Counts against

Divergence persists under verified masking of the channel.

2.8Counts against

E diverges at similar rates under feedback-on and feedback-off blocks.

2.9Counts against

Matching persists undiminished under verified encounter randomisation in a system claimed to be sorting-driven.

2.10Falsifier

An E-free module with Oi clamped still shows an Os effect on E that survives manipulation checks, or two-fixed/one-free contrasts reverse under difference-in-difference tests at the other level of the fixed factor.

2.11Falsifier

Adult host choice alone produces durable change in the host cue field that returns to affect later host choice.

2.12Counts against

The masking-rescue regime fails to suppress and restore Oi divergence, or cross-lagged SEM fails to recover the generating coefficients.

2.13Falsifier

2.15Counts against

Thesis 3. The ruler is part of the phenotype

ClaimKindOutcome
3.1Falsifier

A category-linked size-equivalence shift that disappears under state attenuation (satiation, octopamine block) or under feature matching, or that criterion, gain or lapse models fit as well across ≥3 anchors, refutes f0(C) ≠ 1 for that lineage.

3.2Falsifier

A proposed case fails if nuisance models absorb the effect, feature matching removes it, it fails to generalize across predicted anchors, or it changes response strength without changing the inferred equivalence function. At programme level, independent lineages with opposite cost asymmetries showing same-direction metric shifts count against AMP.

3.3Falsifier

Species tested over an identical layout show no reliable difference in fitted metric weighting n beyond detection differences, or behavioural paths are not predicted by geodesics of any fitted n.

3.4Falsifier

A reliable behavioral change under a verified shared transformation that lies inside the decision’s invariance class would refute it.

3.5Falsifier

If all derived quantities shift by the same log amount, or velocity bias persists when independent estimates give ρ_x = ρ_t, the model fails.

3.6Falsifier

Acceleration judgments under matched space-time gains show no residual bias of -rho_t, and no delta-v model fits better than the velocity-based expression.

3.7Falsifier

If organisms with shifted temporal resolution show no calibration change after contact events (e.g. no update of interception timing after landing or capture), the thesis fails.

3.8Falsifier

Preregistered transfer thresholds are not met, or exposure regime and preferred signal distributions do not covary.

3.9Falsifier

If the experienced distribution is shifted and the reference point does not move, or moves opposite to the prediction, the hypothesis fails in that case.

3.10Counts against

Across ≥3 anchors spanning a preregistered range, a fitted log shift that follows ln((s+α)/s), or remapping failing to beat additive, gain and lapse models by the preregistered ΔAIC/ELPD margin, rejects remapping for that dataset.

3.11Counts against

A demonstrated metric-tuning case in which a lower step (for example, state attenuation) fails while the higher steps hold would show the ordering is wrong.

3.12Counts against

An effect that appears only as higher escape probability or shorter latency, or that fails any of the five go/no-go criteria, is scored as not spatial AMP.

3.13Counts against

A shift that persists after reversible route perturbation, or that encounter history predicts better than receiver state, is classified as not receiver-led.

3.14Counts against

Within the model the result is analytic. Empirically, a lineage with informative routing and a high miss/false-alarm cost ratio that shows no baseline expansion (f0 ≈ 1) after state control counts against the model’s relevance. So does a lineage where a threshold shift alone reaches the same fitness.

3.15Falsifier

If measurement bias is equal in same-category and cross-category conditions, or a global-gain model fits cross-category data as well as remapping, the ruler model fails for that observer.

3.16Falsifier

If population-calibrated pipelines agree across populations as closely as externally calibrated ones, or divergence does not scale with κ, the model fails.

3.17Counts against

A rerun of candidate_B with the shipped code and seeds that yields decisive recovery below 80% or |bias| above 0.03 at ρ_P = 0.139 would overturn the result.

3.18Counts against

If population-trained and ground-truth-trained classifiers show the same area bias across more seeds, the transfer is not supported.

3.19Counts against

If shuffled-fitness, no-selection or policy-only arms reproduce the tight negative bias, the selection explanation fails.

3.20Counts against

A rerun giving false joint support above 5% for further truths, or safe offsets above 0.05, would change the design rule.

4.6Falsifier

4.7Falsifier

Thesis 8. Where one category ends and the next begins

ClaimKindOutcome
8.1Counts against

Repeated measurement shows b_i is not repeatable within individuals; or half-sib or selection-line designs with power at least 0.8 for h2 = 0.1 bound Var_A(b_i) below that value; or male mating success shows no dependence on boundary straddling at matched physical distance (gamma = 0).

8.2Falsifier

A selection-line experiment on a categorical boundary in which the realized response departs from (1-w)^2 sigma2_g beta given independently estimated w and sigma2_g; or a boundary that evolves beyond m under selection on the learning rule alone.

8.3Counts against

A demonstration that a fixed number of common-garden generations removes all listed non-genetic channels in a given system. That would restrict the claim to other systems.

8.4Falsifier

Empirical data in which a single decision-criterion parameter reproduces both the labelling shift and a moved discrimination peak; or a simulation variant (for example a non-Gaussian peak) in which the rule misclassifies boundary shifts as criterion shifts above 5%.

8.5Counts against

No sharp labelling function with a discrimination peak at stage 0; or a sire component whose upper 95% limit excludes h2 = 0.1 at power >= 0.8, together with no response to five generations of divergent selection.

8.6Counts against

A tutor effect equal to the population difference, with no origin effect and no interaction, at power at least 0.9 for w = 0.5, falsifies an intrinsic difference. An origin effect equal to the population difference with no tutor effect falsifies calibration. An interaction identifies the learning rule as the differing phenotype.

8.7Counts against

A duration-coding model with a generalized Weber law (noise s.d. = kt + c) fitted to swamp-sparrow duration discrimination that reproduces failure at 8-to-16 ms and success at 16-to-32 ms without a boundary; or a within-population test in which equal-ratio pairs on one side of the boundary are discriminated unequally.

8.8Falsifier

Joint fitting with an explicit lapse model that fails to remove the centre-ward bias; or repeatability estimated from boundary point estimates that matches latent repeatability at 24 trials and shallow spread.

8.9Falsifier

Any published estimate of repeatability, heritability, twin concordance, selection response or common-garden persistence for the location of a boundary that meets the labelling-plus-discrimination criterion.

Thesis 9. What counts as one

ClaimKindOutcome
9.1Counts against

Raw count predicts early quantity judgements as well as grouping variables do, or cross-modal transfer tracks magnitude rather than shared segmentation.

9.2Falsifier

A validated independent procedure that selects one partition of the track surfaces; or a receiver-mediated selection case in which fitness tracks an analyst-defined unit the receiver does not separate.

9.3Falsifier

Systematic cases in which a change of individuation criterion leaves every downstream estimate and inference invariant, as a unit change does.

9.4Falsifier

Advantage rankings stay the same across opponents and lighting conditions after hunger, experience, terrain and motivation are controlled.

9.5Falsifier

A bisection or magnitude-estimation assay in a signalling receiver that gives an area exponent equal to the length exponent; or a model in which proportional processing alone, with cost and benefit in the same units, bounds exaggeration.

9.6Falsifier

A physical standard that fixes unit counts without an individuation criterion; or a population-fixed individuation shift detected by an instrument without a receiver-defined partition.

9.7Falsifier

Orthogonal manipulation of component area, total projected area, boundary contrast and configuration in which females show component-specific preference at fixed total area.

9.8Falsifier

Animals discriminate event counts reliably at flicker rates above their measured fusion frequency.

9.9Falsifier

Animacy ratings do not change with relevance or control demand when motion statistics are held identical.

9.10Falsifier

Electrosensory tasks in weakly electric fish acquire discrete-count rules as fast as or faster than matched continuous-gradient rules, with stimulus difficulty equated.

9.11Falsifier

Any of the three predictions failing in a species with a characterised perceptual space; for example, matched non-numerical cues leave numerical effects unchanged.

9.12Falsifier

A two-choice design that recovers beta separately from f’; or bisection data whose PSEs across anchor ratios fit no single beta.

9.13Counts against

Reconstruction errors on the known-maker track do not differ across representations. That would show that representation sensitivity does not become reconstruction error.

9.14Falsifier

A null or non-monotone dose-response across surfaces; or a gain in regions accompanied by a change in selected area, which would make the effect a thresholding artefact.

9.15Falsifier

A reanalysis with an independent decoder or human outliners in which along-axis light does not recover more regions, or in which rankings are stable across azimuth.

9.16Counts against

A factorial design that varies small-stimulus size and ratio independently and finds preference tracks the ratio at fixed smaller-male area.

9.17Falsifier

A partition-change rate that is flat or non-monotone across the envelope series, or that vanishes when the same envelopes are applied to raw surfaces of known-maker tracks.

9.18Falsifier

Classification tracks material connectedness, not electrosensory continuity, when the two conflict.

9.19Falsifier

Expectations of the listed relation types appear before the organism individuates the relevant units.

9.20Counts against

No same-target advantage after position exchange with matched final displays, or behaviour fully predicted by location or carrier models.

Thesis 11. Auditing a claim along the path it travelled

ClaimKindOutcome
11.1Counts against

In a sample of retracted or corrected empirical claims, the decisive error lies mostly in data production (fabrication, faulty measurement, statistical error), not in the operational-to-target step.

11.2Counts against

In a sample of cited claims, strength-changing transitions without a named invariance hold up under later tests as often as transitions with one.

11.3Falsifier

A set of cases in which replication along a shared dependency exposed the suspected artifact as often as dependency-diverse replication did.

11.4Counts against

Across historical cosmological claims, peak single-combination significance predicts later confirmation as well as minimum-across-swaps significance does.

11.5Counts against

Across the worked cases, the grammar never classifies a claim differently from current practice and never blocks an inference that was actually made.

11.6Counts against

In the reviewer study, DACF corrected claims converge no better, and match historical resolutions no better, than ordinary prose reviews of the same cases.

11.7Counts against

In the blinded comparison, coders cannot apply dependency groups consistently, dependency maps do not change synthesis conclusions, or a simpler method reaches the same calibration with less burden.

11.8Counts against

Independent coders on a predefined claim sample disagree on target level or output so often that the residual claim is not reproducible (for example, Krippendorff’s α below 0.67 on the output category).

11.9Falsifier

The historical record shows that positive studies used contamination-independent routes (mouse-DNA-free reagents, independent cell lines) and still detected XMRV.

11.10Counts against

In the cross-laboratory design, retrieval for a nominated target weakens under technical separation, or maps to generic stress responses instead of prespecified functional endpoints.

11.11Falsifier

Independent recoding of the 13 records places the narrowings at other transitions, for example at cue-to-stimulus measurement.

11.12Falsifier

One observation or inference route supports all four components at the same grade.

Thesis 12. Science has an Umwelt too

ClaimKindOutcome
12.1Falsifier

A scientific result reaches the community with its content and warrant through no sensory, instrumental, notational or communal step.

12.2Falsifier

A general result shows that mutual-kNN or CKA scores are invariant to gallery composition for systems that share a latent structure but allocate precision differently.

12.3Counts against

Raters cannot assign historical cases a first-moving vertex with inter-rater agreement, or the three components change independently without paradigm change.

12.4Falsifier

The proposition is proved. Its application fails if a purely computational step, with no new coupling, distinguishes two states that the original access map sends to the same output.

12.5Counts against

Route substitutions that keep competence intact but weaken a calibrated relation count against the shared-target explanation for that relation. An unchanged relation under a pre-named constraint change, with the control stable, counts against that Umwelt constraint.

12.6Counts against

Alignment clusters neither tighten nor show hysteresis across successive model generations trained on increasingly model-influenced data.

12.7Counts against

In some measurement domain, residuals correlate with access variables, yet modeling that correlation never changes calibration, attribution or the stated result.

12.8Falsifier

In a benchmark, two routes that the profile marks as Blackwell-ordered on a shared target yield decision performance ordered opposite to the garbling relation.

12.9Counts against

q stays invariant to every evaluation factor (all θ_j = 0 within 95% intervals) and to every route substitution at overlapping competence; then the attribution problem does not arise for q. If q fails stage one, attribution through that pipeline loses its justification.

12.10Falsifier

A demonstration shows that, under realistic lossy observation, the learned kernel still identifies world statistics, and evaluation-batch scores consistently estimate the training kernel.

Thesis 13. An error at the start is not repaired at the end

ClaimKindOutcome
13.1Counts against

Inter-auditor reliability is too low to reproduce target nodes, edge types and continuity statuses. Or the graph detects no more target drift, shortcut learning or aggregation mismatch than provenance or construct-validity review.

13.2Counts against

The formal core follows from the data-processing inequality. The empirical test fails if, in audited pipelines, located cut points do not predict where external validation fails.

13.3Counts against

A retrospective case series in which claims that passed cross-route but not cross-target validation fail no more often than claims that passed both.

13.4Falsifier

In instrument histories or data-intensive fields, target repertoires (ontologies, annotation schemas, claim types) diversify as fast as registered distinctions from the first uses of the instrument.

13.5Falsifier

With a validated dependent variable, randomly sampled concepts show no negative association between pointability score and definitional-dispute rate. Or disputes persist unchanged after an instrument makes a referent bounded and trackable.

13.6Counts against

Audits that record the profile detect no more foreclosed live alternatives or irreversible preprocessing steps than audits without it.

13.7Counts against

A recursive-narrowing audit of reused classifications, observer models or benchmarks finds later agreement explained by independent contact with the target as often as by common descent.

17.3Counts against

17.4Falsifier

17.5Counts against

17.6Falsifier

17.7Falsifier
