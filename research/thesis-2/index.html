Evolution · Thesis 2

Telling an environmental cause from a sensory one

The eco-sensory triangle: route diagnosis by intervention

The thesis

Sensory drive, receiver bias, matching habitat choice and niche construction are constrained subgraphs of one causal graph of cue field E, receiver state Os and interaction phenotype Oi. Holding, masking, encounter-equalising and feedback-toggling interventions decide which route produced a trait-environment match.

The argument in full: Chapter 3. Telling an environmental cause from a sensory one. Every claim below is recorded in Register I.

    Used habitat as a collider Specific extension

    Where an organism is found is an outcome of preference, movement and sensory state, so conditioning analyses on realised habitat use manufactures phenotype-environment associations through collider stratification.

  • 2.3
    Minimal route model with structural zero Operationalisation

    A three-state model X(t) = [E, Os, Oi] in which receiver state reaches E only through Oi (the structural zero ∂E(t+1)/∂Os(t) = 0) generates environment-first, receiver-first, sorting and feedback regimes from one set of equations.

  • 2.5
    Route-priority test Operationalisation

    Receiver-led and sorting-led ordering are distinguished by whether prior receiver state Os(t) or a non-circular exposure proxy better predicts later Oi(t+1) out of sample under matched E, with the causal graph stated before model comparison.

  • 2.6
    Matched-field (flat-E) persistence test Operationalisation

    If Oi divergence persists while the focal cue dimensions of E are held within a declared tolerance band over the decision window, a current-environment explanation of that divergence is rejected for those dimensions.

  • 2.7
    Masking-rescue channel test Operationalisation

    Reversibly masking and then restoring one sensory channel under matched conditions, with sham and general-impairment controls, identifies whether that channel carries Oi divergence; it does not identify the route that produced the divergence.

  • 2.8

  • 2.9
    Encounter-equalisation test Operationalisation

    Randomising settlement or equalising host, habitat, timing and encounter sequence tests sorting: matching or assortative mating that weakens while Os is unchanged was produced by non-random exposure.

  • 2.10
    27-regime factorial intervention grammar Operationalisation

    Holding each of E, Os and Oi high, low or free defines a 3 × 3 × 3 grammar of 27 regimes, of which the twelve two-fixed/one-free regimes are the clean local route tests.

    Experienced cue stream E_exp Terminological proposal

Limitations and open problems

Data

  • No empirical system has been run through all interventions, and no new data were collected at Evolution Canyon (2.2, 2.12, 2.13).

  • The simulation results depend on the model family and the parameter ranges explored; the classifier type is not stated in the register; the numbers are absent from the later submission, and code and seeds must be archived before publication (2.14).

  • Model parameterisations are pedagogical and unfitted (2.4, 2.5).

Inference

  • Cross-lagged panel models confound within- and between-unit effects (Hamaker et al. 2015); parameter recovery depends on handling this (2.5).

  • E_exp = E × sigmoid(Os) in the simulation is a function of Os and cannot serve as independent evidence for Os (2.5, 2.15).

  • Predictive precedence is not causation without the matched-E design, and the route-priority test needs repeated measures of Os and Oi on the same units (2.7).

  • Flat-E persistence leaves heritability, hidden cues and unequal E_exp open (2.8).

  • Multimodal compensation weakens negative masking results, and masking identifies the channel only, not the route (2.9).

  • The durability threshold for feedback must be set per system (2.10).

  • The collider claim is an application of an existing method; the bias arises when use depends on both phenotype and cue field, and its size is system-specific (2.3).

Feasibility and scope

  • Full encounter randomisation is impractical for highly mobile taxa (2.11). Most field systems cannot clamp two variables or realise both high and low states; clamps can leak and impose viability costs (2.13).

  • Sessile Evolution Canyon taxa (plants, soil bacteria) fall outside the collider critique (2.12).

  • ΔM needs manipulation or strong causal assumptions in field systems, and the reporting standard’s value depends on uptake (2.6).
