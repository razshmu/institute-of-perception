Science · Thesis 13

An error at the start is not repaired at the end

Target priority and recursive narrowing

The thesis

Claim-bearing data do not precede target individuation: a scientific claim is the terminal node of a typed dependency graph in which every load-bearing edge carries a target-continuity status (preserved, transformed with bridge, replaced, unresolved).

The argument in full: Chapter 14. An error at the start is not repaired at the end. Every claim below is recorded in Register II.

    Error inheritance: downstream correctness cannot repair upstream misindividuation Specific extension

    If an early assignment maps two world states that differ in the claim-relevant target to the same labelled data, every downstream stage without independent target evidence yields identical output distributions, so no downstream analysis…

  • 13.3
    Cross-target validation as a third level of objectivity Specific extension

    When target identity is disputed, objectivity requires cross-target validation, testing whether a claim survives live alternative target partitions or receiver models, in addition to within-route and cross-route validation.

  • 13.4
    Target-space conservatism hypothesis Testable hypothesis

    The capacity to register relations grows faster than the capacity to formulate targets, so early uses of a new instrument deepen established target classes, and major conceptual advances coincide with target reconstruction.

  • 13.5
    Perceptual pointability predicts definitional disputes in science Testable hypothesis

    Scientific disputes over what counts as one instance concentrate on concepts whose referents human perception does not deliver as bounded, cohesive and persisting objects and that no instrument has converted into such objects.

  • 13.6
    Relation-retention profile of a narrowing step Operationalisation

    Each load-bearing transition in a scientific route is described by a vector, not a scalar: claim-relative risk reduction G(C), foreclosure L(A) for each live alternative, relation-space contraction K, reversibility V, and target status.

Limitations and open problems

  • The edge taxonomy is analytical, not canonical; a single edge can carry several types. (13.1)

  • Neither reliability nor incremental value has been measured. (13.1)

  • The formal core is a direct consequence of known information theory; novelty lies in its use as an audit rule. (13.2)

  • Cut-point identification in real pipelines is untested. (13.2)

  • The supporting paper exists as a rebuild plan, not a finished manuscript. (13.3)

  • Separating biological from cultural inheritance needs comparative and developmental evidence not yet collected. (13.3)

  • ‘Live alternative’ requires domain judgement. (13.4)

  • The case-series test has not been run. (13.4)

  • Registered and targetable domains are operational, not measurable fractions of reality. (13.5)

  • The historical test has not been designed in detail or run. (13.5)

  • All reported correlations (pilot rho = -0.81 and later tests) are void because the outcome counter was invalid. (13.6)

  • Raters were language models; human raters are still required. (13.6)

  • A failed control suggests that experimental tractability co-varies with pointability. (13.6)

  • Valid dispute markers restrict the test to fields that discuss individuation explicitly. (13.6)

  • K and V are defined only over a declared candidate set, not over all possibilities. (13.7)

  • Risk functions must come from the domain; quantification is often only ordinal. (13.7)
