Evolution · Thesis 10

What this should leave in the genome

Receiver-calibration selection and genetic architecture

The thesis

Heritable variation in a few receiver-calibration parameters θ (spectral, temporal, gain) changes many receiver-mediated interactions at once, so selection from any of them loads onto the same parameters: β_θ = Σ_j (∂ln w/∂y_j)(∂y_j/∂θ).

The argument in full: Chapter 11. What this should leave in the genome. Every claim below is recorded in Register I.

Claims

  • 10.1
    Receiver-Calibration Selection Central thesis

    Heritable variation in a few receiver-calibration parameters θ (spectral, temporal, gain) changes many receiver-mediated interactions at once, so selection from any of them loads onto the same parameters: β_θ = Σ_j (∂ln w/∂y_j)(∂y_j/∂θ).

  • 10.2
    Enrichment of receiver-calibration alleles in selectional-pleiotropy space Testable hypothesis

    Receiver-calibration alleles are enriched in the high-magnitude, high-breadth region of the selectional-pleiotropy profile (A_g, B_g, K_g) relative to matched regulatory, neural, endocrine and developmental loci.

  • 10.3
    Brake-and-accelerator tempo hypothesis Testable hypothesis

    A stable receiver-calibration regime buffers the effective environment and yields bounded fluctuation, while rare calibration-regime shifts move many selective optima at once; multi-trait bursts therefore coincide with documented…

  • 10.4
    Perceptual grouping predicts correlational selection Testable hypothesis

    Independently measured perceptual grouping Π_ab of two signal components predicts the correlational selection gradient γ_ab that the receiver imposes, after chemical or physical similarity, biosynthetic pathway and total emission are fitted.

  • 10.5
    Assimilation test for receiver calibration Testable hypothesis

    After multi-generation selection under a stable altered sensory regime, descendants reared in both regimes show a flatter reaction norm of calibration, not only a shifted mean.

  • 10.6
    Receiver-to-G status ledger Operationalisation

    The chain from source properties to genetic covariance has eleven links, each labelled established, unmeasured, non-identifiable, definitional, unknown or conditional, and the weight-bearing untested link is grouping → joint valuation.

Limitations and open problems

  • The components of θ are heterogeneous: spectral tuning has single-site molecular anchors; temporal resolution is a composite measure. (10.1)

  • β_θ is a first-order expression. (10.1)

  • RCS is not itself an indirect genetic effect; it generates IGEs only through plastic partner responses. (10.1)

  • Claims no new mechanism. (10.2)

  • Covariance from shared development, pleiotropy and drift is outside scope. (10.2)

  • The (A, B, K) formulas came from an AI reviewer; priority lies in the enrichment hypothesis. (10.3)

  • B_g depends on how contexts are individuated and is undefined when A_g = 0. (10.3)

  • Candidate-locus designs suffer ascertainment bias; genome-scale or matched panels are needed. (10.3)

  • Receiver transitions have not been shown to explain any fossil pattern. (10.4)

  • Dating sensory-regime transitions on phylogenies is itself uncertain. (10.4)

  • A positive association would not exclude habitat change acting directly on non-receiver traits. (10.4)

  • Grouping need not entail joint valuation (pigeon counter-case). (10.5)

  • Herrera (2001) is uninformative because grouping was not measured. (10.5)

  • G change is conditional on sustained selection. (10.5)

  • Requires rearing descendants in both the ancestral and the altered regime. (10.6)

  • Transgenerational plasticity can mimic flattening unless crossed designs are used. (10.6)

  • Proposed; not run. (10.7)

  • Transfer from restrained PER conditioning to free-flight choice must be shown. (10.7)

  • Informal check, not a systematic literature audit. (10.8)
