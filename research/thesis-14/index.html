Time · Thesis 14

Every system has its own clock

Constructed time and nested clocks

The thesis

Every duration is a count of change against a reference timescale chosen at some layer, and cross-system comparison requires that layer to be declared. Selection shapes each species’ reference timescales, and nested physical systems keep their own clocks where their timescales separate.

The argument in full: Chapter 16. Every system has its own clock. Every claim below is recorded in Register III.

Claims

  • 14.1
    Declared normalization frame for cross-system comparison Central thesis

    Any comparison between systems requires a declared normalization frame; when none is declared, the frame defaults silently to the observer’s own system, as the Earth-orbit year does for cosmic history.

  • 14.2
    Class-specific integration windows (Unified Perceptual Field) Specific extension

    Temporal integration windows are selectable traits, and their calibration by stimulus class splits an organism’s perceptual field into long-integration stable segments and short-integration dynamic segments.

  • 14.3
    Cosmic time as a stack of measurement layers Specific extension

    Humanity’s measured span of cosmic time grew because instruments added measurement layers, and telescopes extend the celestial-cue extraction that animals already use for navigation.

  • 14.4
    Hierarchical adiabatic framing of nested gravitating systems Specific extension

    When ε = t_dyn(x)/t_evo(x+1) ≪ 1, a level-x gravitating system relaxes adiabatically inside the quasi-static potential of its host, and ε indexes this separation across star, galaxy, group and cluster levels.

  • 14.5
    Temporal processing as a selected trait Testable hypothesis

    Selection shapes each species’ temporal processing, and that processing in turn biases which behaviours and niches the lineage can exploit.

  • 14.6
    Cross-species alignment in Θ Testable hypothesis

    Cross-species sensory temporal-resolution data align better in Θ than in seconds when τ_sens is set by 1/CFF or by an allometric metabolic mapping fitted on separate species.

  • 14.7
    Structural convergence at equal internal dynamical age Testable hypothesis

    At fixed halo mass, halos that have lived more internal dynamical times since their last major merger show lower scatter in NFW concentration, with each halo’s clock set by the mean density inside its own scale radius.

  • 14.8
    Quasi-virialisation of collisionless systems Testable hypothesis

    A collisionless self-gravitating system free of continued violent disturbance reaches quasi-virial equilibrium within about ten crossing times.

  • 14.9
    Disk galaxies saturate after a fixed number of orbits Testable hypothesis

    Disk galaxy morphology and bar strength reach a stable, saturated state after about 10–20 internal orbital periods at a characteristic radius, independent of galaxy mass.

  • 14.10
    Commensurate nested cycles Testable hypothesis

    Cycles at adjacent levels (atomic, biological, planetary, galactic) lock at commensurate ratios, so geological and biological cycles correlate with astronomical cycles at those ratios.

  • 14.11
    Flow continuity fails when scale separation fails Testable hypothesis

    Where ε_x ≪ 1 is violated, a smooth time-flow description loses explanatory power and dynamics appear punctuated and multi-scale.

  • 14.12
    Floor and sky: scale position fixes matter and space Testable hypothesis

    Each system registers processes much faster and smaller than its own reference scale as stable matter and processes much slower and larger as a fixed sky.

  • 14.13
    Operational time as stitched layer clocks Operationalisation

    An operational duration is a tick count against a reference timescale defined at a chosen layer, so comparing durations across systems requires a declared normalisation layer.

  • 14.14
    Constructed time Θ and the speed-versus-shape criterion Operationalisation

    An organism’s effective time coordinate is Θ(t) = ∫dt/τ(t); if a context changes only τ, behavioural trajectories coincide in Θ, so any residual shape difference marks changed dynamics rather than changed speed.

  • 14.15
    LLM structural-ladder convergence trial Operationalisation

    A pre-registered, prompt-blinded trial tests whether models from two providers produce ordered cosmic and experiential structural ladders that align beyond shuffled, reversed and unrelated controls.

  • 14.16
    Constructed-time toy simulation Application

    In a toy simulation whose data are generated by a τ-rescaling model, a Level-1 constructed-time model recovers the rescaling in leave-one-context-out validation.

Limitations and open problems

Data and tests

  • No comparative dataset has been assembled for temporal-processing selection, integration windows or Θ alignment. (14.2, 14.5, 14.6)

  • The only quantitative result is a circular toy simulation from a single run without released code. (14.16)

  • The halo, disk, virialisation and adiabatic predictions have not been run on simulation catalogues; the disk paper is a literature synthesis with no new fit. (14.7, 14.8, 14.9, 14.14)

  • The declared-frame numbers are illustrative; the rule states a reporting requirement and does not choose the correct frame. (14.1)

  • A positive LLM ladder result would show a regularity of shared training corpora, not a structure of cosmos or experience. (14.15)

Physics scope

  • CFF measures visual flicker resolution; it is a proxy for sensory integration time only, and interval-timing data need a separate τ. (14.2, 14.6)

  • Proper time and gravitational or kinematic rate differences are distinct from the coarse-graining ratio τ(k,x). (14.13)

  • Concentration depends on mass and epoch (Milky-Way-mass halos c200 ≈ 10, relaxed clusters c200 ≈ 4–5); no single value is mass-independent. Novelty of 14.7 over Ludlow et al. 2014 is small, and an uncited ~10% universal subhalo fraction is not adopted. (14.7)

  • Collisional systems follow different relaxation physics and are excluded from 14.7 and 14.8; a virial ratio within a few percent is not measurable for real clusters, and 14.8 restates established results. (14.7, 14.8)

  • Born-Oppenheimer separation rests on a mass ratio and quantum eigenstates; here it rests on density contrast, so the analogy is structural. Host levels are often not slow (infall onto a cluster runs on the cluster’s own dynamical time), and the framing breaks during major mergers and violent collapse. (14.14)

  • The disk threshold of 10–20 orbits is coarse and depends on the radius chosen: at 15 kpc and 220 km/s one orbit takes about 0.42 Gyr, so a Hubble time is about 33 orbits; a figure of “about 20 outer-disk rotations” holds only at larger radii. (14.9)

  • The “single fundamental beat” is not operationalized, and commensurability tests face look-elsewhere effects. (14.10)

  • The flow-breakdown and floor-and-sky claims have no fixed smoothness or error metric. (14.11, 14.12)

  • Θ-normalization is close to established temporal-scaling analyses; the cross-domain operator is the added part. (14.14)

  • The measured cosmic time scale is a claim within the declared measurement framework; the link to animal navigation is an analogy of function, not a demonstrated mechanism. (14.6)
