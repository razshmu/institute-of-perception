Evolution · Thesis 3

The ruler is part of the phenotype

Adaptive metric perception and measurement inheritance

The thesis

Observers with different time scales can agree on time, speed and acceleration only through a shared third reference, and every physical interaction (with a floor, a mate, a food item) supplies such a local ground truth.

The argument in full: Chapter 4. The ruler is part of the phenotype. Every claim below is recorded in Register I.

Claims

  • 3.1
    Spatial AMP: a heritable baseline calibration term Central thesis

    Perceived extent of an ecologically routed feature class decomposes as d’(C,s) = f0(C)·g(C,s)·d, and the evolutionary claim attaches only to the baseline term f0(C), never to the state term g.

  • 3.2
    Adaptive Metric Perception: selection on equivalence structure Central thesis

    Selection under asymmetric ecological costs tunes the equivalence structure of perception: which physically different stimuli a lineage treats as equal, larger, nearer or faster, not only what enters a channel.

  • 3.3
    Metric equivalence of physical-field and sensory-bias geometry Specific extension

    When a physical field and an organism’s sensory weighting field induce the same conformal metric ds^2 = n(x,y)2(dx2+dy^2), they yield identical Gaussian curvature and geodesics, so path structure alone cannot tell which generated the geometry.

  • 3.4

  • 3.5
    Structured propagation of calibration bias Specific extension

    Under independent multiplicative log-gains ρ_x, ρ_t, ρ_m on extent, duration and mass, a derived quantity q = ∏x_j^a_j carries log bias Σa_jρ_j: velocity ρ_x − ρ_t, kinetic energy ρ_m + 2(ρ_x − ρ_t), time-to-contact ρ_t.

    Interaction creates a local ground truth for cross-species calibration Testable hypothesis

    Observers with different time scales can agree on time, speed and acceleration only through a shared third reference, and every physical interaction (with a floor, a mate, a food item) supplies such a local ground truth.

  • 3.9
    Local calibration to the encountered distribution Testable hypothesis

    Perceptual metrics such as heavy, far and fast are set relative to the distribution of values an organism encounters, so a controlled shift of that distribution moves the reference point by a predicted direction and amount.

  • 3.10
    Multi-anchor discriminator: remapping versus criterion shift Operationalisation

    Metric remapping predicts a log-scale PSE shift that is constant across anchors, while an additive criterion shift predicts a log-scale shift ln((s+α)/s) that falls with the standard s; the two coincide at a single anchor.

    A metric-tuning claim advances through nine ordered evidential steps, and each step names what it rules out and what it leaves open.

Limitations and open problems

  • Unfinished draft with chapter headings and notes (3.1)

  • The ‘bookkeeper’ is not operationalized (3.1)

  • No empirical estimate of f0 exists for any lineage. (3.2)

  • The multiplicative separability of f0 and g is a modelling assumption. (3.2)

  • Heritability of f0 is assumed, not shown. (3.2)

  • The field functional is stated, not solved or simulated. (3.3)

  • Temporal and spatiotemporal metrics are not formalized. (3.3)

  • No lineage has yet been tested. (3.3)

  • 2-D conformal Riemannian model only; no Lorentzian metric or stress-energy coupling. (3.4)

  • The equivalence is mathematical and does not identify a mechanism. (3.4)

  • Evidence for non-Euclidean perceptual space is mixed. (3.4)

  • The claim is a theorem about decision rules; which rule an organism uses must be established for each task. (3.5)

  • Cross-modal consistency pressure can limit shared warps of the body. (3.5)

  • Whole-field recalibration was refuted in review and withdrawn. (3.6)

  • No experiment has been run; the standalone paper is shelved. (3.6)

  • Novelty over Helson (1964) is narrow. (3.7)

  • Bayesian cue-combination accounts explain many such effects without heritable calibration. (3.7)

  • This is a plastic, within-lifetime claim; it is not the AMP hypothesis, which concerns selection. (3.7)

  • Single-class, local analysis; no global or multi-class optimum. (3.8)

  • Gaussian size distributions and quadratic maintenance cost are conveniences. (3.8)

  • The ‘symmetric-cost’ regime also changes routing (0.70/0.30), so it does not isolate cost symmetry. (3.8)

  • The model does not compare remapping against a jointly optimized threshold θ. (3.8)

  • The result is analytic given the multiplicative model. (3.9)

  • The ‘axiom checks’ are observer-consistency checks, not violations of physics. (3.9)

  • Fitness rewards displacement of perceived size directly (W = exp(d·k·log(s_eff/s_true))), so divergence of ρ is built in. (3.10)

  • The κ relation is linear by construction; the tabled values are illustrative. (3.10)

  • Model recovery identifies direction, not magnitude; M2, M3, M5 and M6 were never recovered. (3.10)

  • Derived, not measured. (3.11)

  • Assumes multiplicative, independent, log-linear gains. (3.11)

  • An earlier derivation gave acceleration as ρ_x − ρ_t and force as ρ_m + ρ_x − ρ_t; for a = x/t² the correct values are ρ_x − 2ρ_t and ρ_m + ρ_x − 2ρ_t. (3.11)

  • Assumes independent multiplicative log-gains per primitive. (3.12)

  • The looming-proxy case is not given in closed form. (3.12)

  • Receiver Spatial Tuning itself is not yet established. (3.12)

  • Separation grows with anchor range; over 0.8–1.25 the difference is at most about 0.03 log units. (3.13)

  • Gain (M3) and lapse (M4) are weakly identified in the archived design (45.0% and 8.3% recovery). (3.13)

  • A criterion shift proportional to the standard mimics remapping. (3.13)

  • The ladder is a standard of inference, not an empirical result. (3.14)

  • The ordering of heritable response and neural locus is a choice. (3.14)

  • Proposed; not run. (3.15)

  • Size-equivalence psychophysics in flies needs a validated matching or two-alternative readout. (3.15)

  • Depends on the eco-sensory-triangle claims. (3.16)

  • No system yet implements all four contrasts. (3.16)

  • Simulation of the author’s own generative model; says nothing about biological occurrence. (3.17)

  • 90% interval coverage was not computed at the canonical cell (Table S7b shows ‘—’), so the strict coverage criterion is untested there. (3.17)

  • Recovery is weak below ρ_P ≈ 0.1. (3.17)

  • Five seeds; p ≈ 0.04. (3.18)

  • Labels are defined by each population’s own threshold, so transfer follows partly by construction. (3.18)

  • The ground-truth-trained classifier itself overestimates area by 0.79. (3.18)

  • Populations A and B were evolved under opposite cost asymmetries, yet Pass 2 found asymmetry has no effect on bias. (3.18)

  • Attribution to passage and foraging is by elimination; no arm removes those tasks. (3.19)

  • Environment invariance of the bias is by construction, because the rod stimuli do not change between environments. (3.19)

  • One model architecture. (3.19)

  • The offset grid is coarse (0, 0.02, 0.05,…), so the true tolerance lies between 0.02 and 0.05. (3.20)

  • The hierarchical stress test (Pass 7) used only 8 datasets; its two-stage per-fly fit had bias −0.031 and 50% coverage. (3.20)

  • Identifiability only; not evidence that AMP occurs. (3.20)
