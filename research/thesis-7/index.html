Evolution · Thesis 7

What selection acts on

The interactor: organism–field coupling as the unit selection acts on

The thesis

Within the scientific description, the interactor in Hull’s sense is the organism coupled to its perceived and enacted field. The replicator is the heritable receiver architecture that draws that field.

The argument in full: Chapter 8. What selection acts on. Every claim below is recorded in Register I.

Claims

  • 7.1
    The organism–field coupling is the interactor Central thesis

    Within the scientific description, the interactor is the organism coupled to its perceived and enacted field, and the replicator is the heritable receiver architecture.

Limitations and open problems

Replicator and inheritance

  • Ecological inheritance (Odling-Smee et al. 2003) passes niche conditions to descendants, but it lacks the high-fidelity copying Hull requires of a replicator. This register treats it as an inheritance channel alongside the replicator, not part of it. (7.1)

Evidence

  • No data yet estimate selection on the coupled organism–field description; the model comparison in the test is unrun (7.1).

  • The coupled descriptions the test relies on are themselves hypotheses and lack empirical estimates (7.1).

Scope

  • The field is defined only within the declared measurement protocol. The claim says nothing about an environment as it is apart from access, and makes no metaphysical claim; those options belong to a separate, non-deposited map of metaphysical options (7.1).
