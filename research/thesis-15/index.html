Time · Thesis 15

Why a system cannot fully see itself

Nested receivers, shared cores and the limits of self-representation

The thesis

Every record, including a system’s record of itself, is formed by a receiver with its own selectivity, so its standing depends on the receiver architecture, the declared state space and the route by which it was obtained. Shared inferential cores, layered measurement chains and self-models are three cases of this one rule.

The argument in full: Chapter 17. Why a system cannot fully see itself. Every claim below is deposited in Register III.

    Nested receivers in quantum measurement Central thesis

    Quantum measurement is a layered chain of record formation through six receiver classes, each with its own success criterion; the framework addresses record-basis selection and record efficacy, not unique outcomes.

  • 15.3
    Four regimes of self-representation: cell, route and bridge Central thesis

  • 15.4
    Single-core multi-port perception Specific extension

    One inferential core coupled to several sensory ports out-predicts independent per-port models per unit complexity and shows cross-port compensation proportional to port precision.

  • 15.5
    Shared-core / local-overlay architecture Testable hypothesis

    Under incomplete local access, a shared inferential core with agent-specific overlays out-predicts fully local models at moderate-to-high task overlap, until coordination cost or common-mode failure cost exceeds the gain.

  • 15.6
    Layer-conservation gradient in rendered perception Testable hypothesis

    Shared lower perceptual layers are more conserved across taxa than higher layers, which become progressively taxon-specific.

  • 15.7
    Receiver-category test for detectors Operationalisation

    With prepared input fixed and detector implementations varied, a pre-specified receiver-category signature predicts readout class beyond source-only and device-specific models.

  • 15.8
    FGL no-go and Wolpert self-simulation reconciled Application

    The conflict between the Fields–Glazebrook–Levin (2024) no-go theorems and Wolpert’s (2025) self-simulation lemma dissolves on three distinctions: generic versus special systems, inductive versus constructed self-models, and fixed versus…

  • 15.9
    Completeness is indexed to a state space fixed before the evidence Terminological proposal

    A self-representation is complete only relative to a state space Σ and precision ε declared before the evidence is examined, and narrowing Σ after inspection changes the claim’s regime instead of completing it.

  • 15.10
    Receiver-dependent selectivity Terminological proposal

    Every record-forming receiver has a coupling profile, a sensitivity range, a set of stable distinctions and a record threshold, so the form of a record depends in part on the receiver’s organisation.

  • 15.11
    Record versus cue Terminological proposal

    A record is the stabilised state formed at one stage of the measurement chain, and a cue is the role that state plays for a later receiver, so downstream receivers inherit records rather than the upstream event.

Limitations and open problems

Evidence and data

  • No simulation, protocol or dataset exists for the architecture and detector claims (15.3, 15.4, 15.8).

  • The auditor-utility test of the regime triple and the error-rate effect of declaring Σ in advance are untested (15.1, 15.7).

  • The layer-conservation gradient is stated in one sentence with no formal model (15.6).

Scope of novelty

  • Precision-proportional compensation is established in human cue combination; only the architectural selection result is new (15.4).

  • Early-circuit conservation is known; the novelty is its derivation from redundancy avoidance (15.6).

  • The receiver chain sits close to the von Neumann chain and quantum Darwinism; receiver-dependent selectivity is einselection plus detector response under one name; record versus cue is terminological (15.6, 15.10, 15.11).

  • The FGL–Wolpert work is a reconciliation by factorisation, not a new theorem; the general point that computation needs inputs is old (15.1, 15.8).

Formal gaps

  • The two capacity bounds are analogous pigeonhole arguments, not a proven equivalence. The unification by a quantum-reference-frame dimension that grows with iteration count is a separate conjecture (T151b) (15.9).

  • QRF objects cannot be imported into Wolpert’s formalism without an explicit correspondence; the cross-formalism convergence on synchronous completeness is a conjecture (15.2, 15.7).

  • Lag and partiality are continuous in real systems; the four corners are limiting cases (15.7).

  • The completeness rule does not say which Σ fits a given scientific question (15.1).

Excluded scope

  • The measurement framework does not address the definite-outcome problem (15.2).

  • Unity-of-consciousness implications and the variant “across isolated organisms” are outside scope (15.3, 15.4).

  • The label “physical subjectivity” is retired as open to misreading (15.10).
