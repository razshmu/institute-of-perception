Evolution · Thesis 1

A sensory change can start a divergence

Natural Perception Hypothesis and receiver-first evolution

The thesis

Natural selection acts on heritable perceptual processing, and one change in that processing changes which differences an organism can act on across contexts. A heritable receiver change can therefore start ecological divergence, and receiver-first routes are decided system by system by stated abandonment conditions.

The argument in full: Chapter 2. A sensory change can start a divergence. Every claim below is recorded in Register I.

Claims

  • 1.1
    Natural Perception Hypothesis (NPH) Central thesis

    Natural selection acts on perceptual processing (the weighting, integration and stabilisation of sensory signals), and a heritable change in that processing changes which differences an organism can act on across contexts.

  • 1.2
    Receiver-first evolution (RFE) Central thesis

    A heritable change in receiver sensory state, such as an opsin or odorant-receptor variant, starts divergence in mate choice and habitat preference while the focal cue field is held constant.

  • 1.3
    Perception Ecology research programme Central thesis

    Perception Ecology is a research programme that studies how heritable, species-specific perceptual processing shapes ecological interaction and adaptive radiation, with the organism’s internal perceptual state as its first component.

  • 1.4
    Scope conditions for receiver-first routes Specific extension

  • 1.5
    Receiver-side pleiotropy: sensory-biased magic traits Specific extension

    One change in receiver perception couples ecological performance and mate choice, so the pleiotropy of a sensory-biased magic trait resides in the receiver rather than in the sender’s trait genes.

  • 1.6
    Primate trichromacy as candidate receiver-first loop Testable hypothesis

    Primate trichromacy is a candidate receiver-first loop: an L/M opsin change improved red-green discrimination, shifted fruit choice, altered seed dispersal, and so changed selection on fruit colour and on colour vision.

  • 1.7
    Sensory-contrast restoration as conservation lever Testable hypothesis

    Where turbidity, artificial light or noise collapses the sensory discrimination that maintains divergence, restoring sensory contrast protects the diverged populations more directly than managing habitat area.

    NPH amplification formalism Operationalisation

    Because one sensory feature S enters the perception of every environmental trait (P_i = φ(S)·E_i), a small change in S shifts all perceived traits together and scales the selection gradient on S with the number of traits it touches.

Limitations and open problems

Data and tests

  • No empirical system yet satisfies all three links of the receiver-first motif under intervention, and no field survey of candidate systems exists (1.2, 1.8).

  • No direct data link a single processing variant to diversification; the gene-edit design is unrun, and one edit tests initiation of behavioural divergence, not speciation (1.1, 1.4, 1.13).

  • The feedback link from primate vision to fruit colour is inferential; tetrachromatic bird dispersers plausibly shaped fruit colour before primate trichromacy (1.5).

  • No field comparison of contrast restoration against habitat management exists; the lever applies only where masking-rescue is shown for the system (1.6).

  • The same-signed selection in receiver-side pleiotropy is argued, not modelled, and overlaps the sensory-bias literature (1.7).

  • The amplification term P_i = φ(S)·E_i is a stipulation; an “orders of magnitude” amplification is unsupported, and the comparative predictions are open to confounds (1.9).

  • ninaE edits may not change flicker-fusion frequency cleanly; the test therefore adds controls and mate-choice and habitat endpoints (1.13).

Scope and operationalisation

  • “Without environmental change” is operationalised as matched focal cue dimensions; unmeasured cue dimensions remain open (1.4, 1.13).

  • The published “whole experienced environment” wording was narrowed; the defensible core is the cross-context reach of one processing change (1.1).

  • Loop-model parameters are illustrative and unfitted; the November 2025 simulation is environment-first; the model is superseded by 2.4 (1.10).

  • Perception Ecology overlaps established sensory ecology, and not all four components are yet specified (1.12).

  • The perceptual-niche term is superseded in technical work by E_exp (1.14). Decline and ascent are terms only (1.15).
