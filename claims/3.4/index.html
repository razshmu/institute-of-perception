3.4 Metric leg and the transparency principle

Claim

A shared transformation of body and environment leaves unchanged every decision whose scale type it preserves: strictly increasing maps preserve ordinal decisions, positive affine maps preserve interval and norm-referenced decisions, and similarity maps preserve ratio decisions.

Receiver variation has behavioral consequences only through transformations outside the invariance class of the decision. These include body–scene differentials, anisotropy, region-specific gain, cross-modal mismatch, and nonlinear warps applied to ratio, difference or absolute-threshold decisions. A null result under a verified shared transformation of the right class supports the invariance argument.

What is new

Measurement theory (Stevens 1946; Krantz et al. 1971) defines invariance by scale type; prism and curvature adaptation show perceived geometry can be warped and recalibrated. This claim turns the objection ‘uniform distortion is undetectable’ into a design rule for which receiver differences can matter.

Prior art

Stevens 1946 (added); Krantz, Luce, Suppes & Tversky 1971 (added); Gibson 1933; Redding et al. 2005; Akre et al. 2011.

Test and evidence

Metric-leg derivation, corrected in the AMP supplement and in the CPR specification §3.1 and §3.4.

Falsifier

A reliable behavioral change under a verified shared transformation that lies inside the decision’s invariance class would refute it.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
