1.5 Receiver-side pleiotropy: sensory-biased magic traits

Claim

One change in receiver perception couples ecological performance and mate choice, so the pleiotropy of a sensory-biased magic trait resides in the receiver rather than in the sender’s trait genes.

Classical magic traits need pleiotropy or tight linkage in the sender’s trait. A perceptual shift acts on foraging and mate preference at once and gives same-signed selection on the trait axis. Swapping the sensory system between populations then transfers both preference and performance.

What is new

Rodd et al. 2002 show guppy orange preference as a foraging by-product; Servedio et al. 2011 and Maan & Seehausen 2012 define magic traits and cues. This locates the pleiotropy in the receiver as a general route and gives a transfer test. It is the parent claim of the reporting standard 2.6.

Prior art

Rodd et al. 2002; Servedio et al. 2011; Maan & Seehausen 2012.

Test and evidence

Transfer of the sensory system between populations by hybrids or transgenics.

Falsifier

Swapping the sensory system between populations does not transfer both the preference and the performance difference.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
