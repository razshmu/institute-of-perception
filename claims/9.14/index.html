9.14 Low-frequency attenuation adds regions to footprint partitions in a dose-dependent way

Claim

On 37 published track surfaces, attenuating low spatial frequencies with a filter pair identical above a transition frequency increases the number of recovered regions on 28 surfaces and decreases it on none, and the increase grows with attenuation on 36 of 37.

A raised-cosine shelf passes S(f) = 1 above f_t and attenuates below it by depth a; its control twin is identical above f_t. The design crosses 3 depths, 3 transitions, 2 cutoffs, 3 decoder quantiles and 3 noise seeds (162 conditions per surface, 5994 matched pairs). The median surface gains one region (sign test p = 7.45 x 10^-9) with selected area unchanged. The within-surface rank correlation between attenuation and gain is positive on 36 of 37 surfaces (p = 5.5 x 10^-10).

What is new

Lallensack (2019) and Lallensack, Romilio & Falkingham (2022) show that outlines vary between observers and renderings. New: isolation of low-frequency transmission with a matched filter pair and a dose parameter in a fully crossed design, with the surface as the unit of replication.

Prior art

Lallensack 2019; Lallensack, Romilio & Falkingham 2022; Hartmann et al. 2026 (DinoTracker); Hiller & Smith 2008.

Test and evidence

Footprint partition-filter analysis, section 5 (shelf experiment). 28/37, 0/37 and 36/37.

Falsifier

A null or non-monotone dose-response across surfaces; or a gain in regions accompanied by a change in selected area, which would make the effect a thresholding artefact.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
