8.8 Two estimation biases for boundary location

Claim

Fixing the lapse rate at zero pulls estimated boundaries toward the centre of the stimulus range at any trial number, and treating estimated boundaries as data attenuates repeatability by up to 37% in the simulated conditions, so latent boundaries must be fitted jointly with the psychometric response.

With 6% true lapses and lapse fixed at zero, the slope of estimation error on true location was -0.10 at 192 trials, against about 0 with lapse estimated, and edge error was six times larger (0.078 vs 0.013). A slope of -0.10 shrinks among-individual variance by about a fifth. In the repeatability simulation (400 replicates per cell), naive repeatability fell to 0.316 at true R = 0.5 with 24 trials and shallow spread. Significance tests were nearly unaffected.

What is new

Lapse-induced bias in psychometric fitting (Wichmann & Hill 2001; Schutt et al. 2016) and attenuation by measurement error are known. New: magnitudes specific to boundary location, and the consequence that cross-study or cross-population boundary comparisons need a common stimulus range and an explicit lapse model, which the New York versus Pennsylvania sparrow comparison lacks.

Prior art

Wichmann & Hill 2001; Schutt et al. 2016; Kruuk 2004.

Test and evidence

Simulations S1 (boundary recovery) and S2 (repeatability); receiver-boundary article, sections 5a and 7.

Falsifier

Joint fitting with an explicit lapse model that fails to remove the centre-ward bias; or repeatability estimated from boundary point estimates that matches latent repeatability at 24 trials and shallow spread.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
