13.1 Target Priority Principle and the typed target-to-claim graph

Claim

Claim-bearing data do not precede target individuation: a scientific claim is the terminal node of a typed dependency graph in which every load-bearing edge carries a target-continuity status (preserved, transformed with bridge, replaced, unresolved).

Records become data of X for claim C only through operations that individuate a unit, maintain its identity, bind properties to it and place it in relations. Edges are typed as constitutive, enabling, realizational, functional substitution, transformative or epistemic bridge. A target change is not a flaw; an unmarked target change is. The graph applies across avian colour science, EHT imaging and radiographic machine learning.

What is new

The prior art locates overinterpretation at the product-to-target transition. This version follows target identity through every transition from signal to claim, adds a four-value continuity status and a six-type edge taxonomy, and imports object-file and FINST distinctions (indexing, tracking, binding) into scientific target formation.

Prior art

Bogen & Woodward 1988; Leonelli 2015; Tal 2017; Pylyshyn 1989; Kahneman, Treisman & Gibbs 1992; Kane 2013; W3C PROV provenance models.

Counts against

Inter-auditor reliability is too low to reproduce target nodes, edge types and continuity statuses. Or the graph detects no more target drift, shortcut learning or aggregation mismatch than provenance or construct-validity review.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
