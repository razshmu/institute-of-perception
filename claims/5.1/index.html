Evolution · Thesis 5 · Central thesis

5.1 Receiver-relative trait reorganization (RRT)

Claim

A measured change in a receiver’s sensory-organizational mapping (R, Π) reorganizes the task-relative units through which existing physical variation affects action, creating new dimensions of selection before matching physical traits evolve.

Selection acts on heritable reaction-norm parameters that generate the partition, not on the acquired partition state. The physical variation is unchanged; what changes is which differences make a difference to the receiver’s action. The units and relations are defined within the declared measurement protocol.

What is new

Sensory bias and sensory drive show receivers shaping sender traits along existing dimensions. RRT adds that receiver change creates new task-relative units, and makes that claim testable (5.6, 5.8).

Prior art

Ryan 1990; Endler 1992; Ryan & Cummings 2013; West-Eberhard 2003.

Test and evidence

The task-equivalence test (5.6) and the combined causal test (5.8).

Falsifier

Independently measured receiver change fails to predict the equivalence structure; or a simpler sensitivity, criterion, exposure or motor model predicts it as well; or the receiver pathway adds nothing to the combined model.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
