8.5 Drosophila serrata boundary protocol

Claim

We specify a Drosophila serrata contact-pheromone protocol that first tests whether a categorical boundary exists and then estimates its heritability in a family-level half-sib design of about 10^4 singly tested females, followed by divergent selection on boundary location.

Stage 0 builds a chemically verified synthetic blend series on one declared pheromone axis and requires a sharp labelling function with a discrimination peak at its midpoint; without both, the programme stops. Stage 1 fits dam-family psychometric functions and a nested sire/dam model with known sampling variances. In simulation, 9600 females (80 sires, 120 daughters each) gave power 0.58 and 19,200 gave 0.90 for h2 = 0.2. Stage 2 applies five generations of divergent selection on family boundary, with spread and lapse as covariates.

What is new

Delcourt, Blows & Rundle (2010) and Rundle et al. (2010) estimate genetic variance in D. serrata preference for contact pheromones. New: a boundary-existence gate, a family-level psychometric design with known sampling variances, and a stated selection test for boundary location in this system.

Prior art

Delcourt, Blows & Rundle 2010; Rundle et al. 2010; Nelson & Marler 1989; Kruuk 2004.

Test and evidence

Simulations S3 and S3b (half-sib power analysis); receiver-boundary article, section 6b.

Counts against

No sharp labelling function with a discrimination peak at stage 0; or a sire component whose upper 95% limit excludes h2 = 0.1 at power >= 0.8, together with no response to five generations of divergent selection.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
