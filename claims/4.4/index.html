4.4 Organismal regulation preserves its own regulating process

Claim

Stars and organisms both regulate; a star’s feedback preserves a balance of forces, while an organism’s feedback preserves the conditions without which the regulating process itself stops.

Core fusion feedback keeps a star stable for billions of years, so “has regulation” does not separate stars from organisms. In the organism the loop is closed on itself: what is preserved is the capacity to go on preserving. This gives “failure” a meaning, a state of no return that the system acts in relation to. The contrast orders four regimes (star or stone, soil, rooted life, moving life) without a threshold.

What is new

Corrects the common claim that stars lack regulation. The self-maintenance criterion is close to organizational closure; the added elements are the star contrast and its use to grade four regimes along a continuum.

Prior art

Maturana & Varela (autopoiesis); Jonas 1966; Di Paolo 2005; Moreno & Mossio 2015.

Test and evidence

Conceptual contrast; no empirical test specified.

Falsifier

A non-living system whose feedback preserves the conditions of its own regulating process in the same closed way.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
