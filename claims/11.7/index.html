11.7 Microscopy claim-dependency record

Claim

The microscopy claim-dependency record rewrites an imaging conclusion into minimal propositions and passes each through two gates: whether the image operation is validated for its measurement task, and which biological proposition that validated operation warrants.

The conclusion splits into operational result, target attribution, relation or state, functional effect and mechanism, with scope as a qualifier. A method-neutral comparison target is fixed before evidence is weighed. Only dependencies whose change could alter a component’s disposition are retained, each tagged by route layer and diagnostic role. Missing information is recorded as missing, never as evidence, and components receive separate, non-compensatory dispositions.

What is new

QUAREP-LiMi, REMBI and Metrics Reloaded standardize instrument performance, metadata and image-analysis validation. The record adds a formal split of measurement validity (Gate 1) from biological warrant (Gate 2), a disposition-changing retention rule that keeps maps finite, and proposition-identity classes (same, narrower, broader, nested, scope-shifted, different, indeterminate) for cross-study comparison.

Prior art

Kane 2013; Messick 1989; Campbell & Fiske 1959; QUAREP-LiMi; REMBI; Metrics Reloaded (Maier-Hein et al. 2024); W3C PROV; PRISMA/SWiM; Algar et al. 2019; Stegenga & Menon 2017.

Test and evidence

Worked cases: Moshkov et al. learned representations; Wilke et al. 2021 route table. Proposed blinded comparison against narrative review measuring incremental value, coder burden, granularity sensitivity and updating.

Counts against

In the blinded comparison, coders cannot apply dependency groups consistently, dependency maps do not change synthesis conclusions, or a simpler method reaches the same calibration with less burden.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
