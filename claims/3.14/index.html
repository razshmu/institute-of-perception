3.14 Local invasion condition for spatial remapping

Claim

In a signal-detection model with imperfect routing (r_P > r_N), remapping f > 1 invades from neutrality exactly when λπ_P r_P E_P[dφ((d−θ)/σ)] exceeds κπ_N r_N E_N[dφ((d−θ)/σ)].

Stimuli routed to the predator channel yield evidence f·d + ε, other stimuli d + ε, and the organism responds when evidence exceeds θ. Differentiating expected fitness at f = 1 gives the invasion gradient: miss-cost benefit on routed predators minus false-alarm cost on misrouted neutrals. The quadratic maintenance cost has zero slope at f = 1, so it sets the size of the equilibrium f, not the sign of invasion. Under the archived parameters, f is 1.54, 1.32 and 1.30 in three asymmetric regimes and 0.92 in the symmetric-cost regime.

What is new

Signal detection theory and the smoke-detector principle give threshold shifts under asymmetric costs; error management theory makes the same point for judgment bias. This model places the asymmetry on a multiplicative extent gain within a routed channel. It gives the invasion condition in terms of routing fidelity, encounter rate and cost ratio.

Prior art

Green & Swets 1966; Nesse 2005 (smoke-detector principle); Haselton & Buss 2000 (error management); Dall et al. 2005.

Test and evidence

Analytic gradient matches finite differences (0.713293 both ways), and equilibria are verified as maxima, in the validation report; regime table in AMP SI Table S1.

Counts against

Within the model the result is analytic. Empirically, a lineage with informative routing and a high miss/false-alarm cost ratio that shows no baseline expansion (f0 ≈ 1) after state control counts against the model’s relevance. So does a lineage where a threshold shift alone reaches the same fitness.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
