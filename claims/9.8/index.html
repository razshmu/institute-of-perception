Evolution · Thesis 9 · Testable hypothesis

9.8 Event number is indexed to temporal grain

Claim

The number of events in a sequence has no receiver-free value: a 40 Hz flicker is forty events per second for a receiver whose flicker-fusion frequency exceeds 40 Hz and one steady light for a receiver whose fusion frequency is lower.

Counting objects needs a spatial partition, and counting events needs a temporal partition. The temporal partition has a measured parameter, flicker-fusion frequency, that differs between species and individuals. A report of how many events occurred is therefore complete only when it names the temporal grain. Detecting flicker and resolving it into countable events are separate capacities, and fusion frequency measures only the first.

What is new

Flicker-fusion comparative work (Healy et al. 2013; Inger et al. 2014) treats CFF as temporal resolution. This claim uses it as a direct, quantitative case that the number of events is receiver-indexed, and separates flicker detection from event individuation.

Prior art

Healy et al. 2013 (CFF across species); Inger et al. 2014; Feigenson, Dehaene & Spelke 2004

Test and evidence

Present flicker trains near a species’ CFF in an event-count discrimination task; count discrimination should fail at rates where flicker detection still succeeds.

Falsifier

Animals discriminate event counts reliably at flicker rates above their measured fusion frequency.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
