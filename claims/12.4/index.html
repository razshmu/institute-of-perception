12.4 Access map and access partition

Claim

An access procedure defines a map from world states to access products; the states it cannot tell apart form equivalence classes, no processing of its output splits those classes, and splitting a class requires a new coupling to the world.

An access procedure P fixes observer, task, medium, instrument, calibration, representation and error model. It composes coupling, transduction and processing into a map A_P from world states W to an output space X_P. Setting w1 ~ w2 when A_P(w1) = A_P(w2) gives an equivalence relation and a partition W/~_P. Any function of the output is constant on each cell, so post-processing cannot refine the partition; instruments refine it only by adding a new coupling, and they reorganize mediation rather than remove it.

What is new

The construction is the standard kernel (quotient) of a function, as in indiscernibility relations and rough sets; the mathematics is not new. The claim uses it as the formal core of species-specific access: animal Umwelten and instrument-extended human access are compared as partitions over a shared state space, with instruments as partition-refining couplings.

Prior art

Quotient and indiscernibility constructions; Pawlak 1982; data-processing inequality (Cover & Thomas 2006); von Uexküll 1934; Hacking 1983; van Fraassen 2008.

Test and evidence

Elementary proof in Formalization Section 0707 (Propositions 1 and 3).

Falsifier

The proposition is proved. Its application fails if a purely computational step, with no new coupling, distinguishes two states that the original access map sends to the same output.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
