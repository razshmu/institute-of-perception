1.15 Perceptual decline and ascent

Claim

Perceptual decline is the narrowing, coarsening or excessive cost of a range of differences formerly available to a lineage, and perceptual ascent is the appearance or sharpening of a discrimination. Both are defined on a stated axis and do not rank organisms.

The terms describe change along one discrimination axis, such as light sensitivity in cavefish. Loss on one axis can coincide with ascent on another, such as lateral-line sensitivity. No overall scale of progress follows.

What is new

Regressive-evolution work (Jeffery 2009; Yoshizawa et al. 2010) documents eye loss with mechanosensory gain. This adds axis-relative, non-ranking terms usable across modalities.

Prior art

Jeffery 2009; Yoshizawa et al. 2010, 2014; Bilandžija et al. 2020; Moran et al. 2023.

Test and evidence

Applied to Astyanax cavefish in a book-length treatment in preparation.

Counts against

The usage fails if decline and ascent on stated axes cannot be measured independently of each other.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
