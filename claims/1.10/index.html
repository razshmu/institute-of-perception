1.10 Sensory-feedback loop model (RFE)

Claim

A path-coefficient loop model gives each link a named coefficient (Os→Oi γ, Oi→E α, E→Os and E→Oi returns, Oi→Os recalibration, Os inheritance via h² and β). “Which route operates” then becomes “which coefficients are non-zero”, estimable from designed toggles.

The model encodes sensory drive, receiver-driven niche construction and sorting as settings of one coefficient set. A generational simulation parameterised on Lake Victoria Pundamilia illustrates it: modest initial Os divergence reorganises Oi and, with α > 0, shifts E onto a new trajectory. The simulation illustrates the model; it is not evidence about cichlids. The current form is 2.4.

What is new

Quantitative-genetic and eco-evolutionary models (Lande & Arnold 1983; Post & Palkovacs 2009; Govaert et al. 2019) model trait-environment feedback, and path analysis (Wright 1921) gives coefficient notation. This adds a receiver-state node and maps named motifs to coefficient patterns.

Prior art

Wright 1921; Lande & Arnold 1983; Falconer & Mackay 1996; Post & Palkovacs 2009; Govaert et al. 2019.

Test and evidence

Generational simulation (DASH working paper 2025-07-17); estimation from toggle designs.

Counts against

Os has low repeatability or heritability; initial Os fails to predict change in Oi under matched E; full-loop models do not outperform reduced ones in intact-loop blocks.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.

Thesis 1: Chapter 2. A sensory change can start a divergence
