9.15 Illumination azimuth changes and reorders footprint partitions

Claim

On 37 track surfaces, illumination azimuth alone changes the recovered region count on every surface (median span x2.20, up to x7.0), light along the specimen’s principal axis recovers more regions on 31 of 37, and specimen rankings reverse in 26.2% of surface-pair comparisons.

Across eight azimuths the count differs on all 37 surfaces, across altitude, vertical exaggeration and decoder threshold, with selected area unchanged. The along-versus-across contrast was fixed by geometry before counting: Wilcoxon p = 9.2 x 10^-7, median ratio 1.36. It survives leaving out each of nine localities and a locality cluster bootstrap (95% CI on the median difference +1.94 to +3.11). Of 335,664 surface-pair comparisons across azimuth pairs, 26.2% reverse order.

What is new

Lallensack (2019) documents rendering dependence qualitatively, and hillshade practice fixes one azimuth. New: quantified azimuth dependence, a direction effect specified in advance with locality-clustered inference, and ranking reversals that bear on comparative ichnology.

Prior art

Lallensack 2019; standard hillshade rendering practice.

Test and evidence

Footprint partition-filter analysis, section 6; companion analysis of individuation without triangulation; illumination analysis (2.20x, range 1.62-7.00) and rank-reversal analysis (26.2%).

Falsifier

A reanalysis with an independent decoder or human outliners in which along-axis light does not recover more regions, or in which rankings are stable across azimuth.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
