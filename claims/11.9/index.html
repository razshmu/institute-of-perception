11.9 XMRV/ME-CFS worked reconstruction

Claim

A DACF reconstruction of XMRV and ME/CFS keeps the operational history, that access chains produced XMRV-like signals more often in patient samples, and refuses the infection claim, because admissible contamination routes were never separated from infection.

The original chain produced XMRV-like sequences and antibody signals. Admissible routes included mouse DNA and reagent contamination and a laboratory-derived recombinant virus. Replication shared those routes, so it did not contain them. Blinded multicentre testing then failed to detect XMRV in patients; the corrected claim reports the signals and their route and refuses infection.

What is new

Hue et al. 2010, Paprotka et al. 2011 and Alter et al. 2012 established the contamination explanation. The reconstruction shows that DACF reaches the historical verdict at the claim level, keeps the operational finding and identifies shared-route replication as the robustness failure.

Prior art

Lombardi et al. 2009 (retracted 2011); Hue et al. 2010; Paprotka et al. 2011; Alter et al. 2012; Delviks-Frankenberry et al. 2012.

Test and evidence

Reconstruction against the published historical record in the DACF final manuscript.

Falsifier

The historical record shows that positive studies used contamination-independent routes (mouse-DNA-free reagents, independent cell lines) and still detected XMRV.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
