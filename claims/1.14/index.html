Evolution · Thesis 1 · Terminological proposal

1.14 Perceptual niche

Claim

A perceptual niche is the set of cue differences a given organism can register and act on; one measured habitat supports several perceptual niches, and a sensory variant defines a new one without any change in the measured habitat.

The perceptual niche is a niche dimension distinct from the resource-based niche. It is defined relative to the observer-measured cue field and the organism’s discrimination capacities. Two variants in one habitat occupy different perceptual niches when they differ in which cue differences they act on. Later technical work replaces the term with the experienced cue stream E_exp (2.15).

What is new

Umwelt names species-specific worlds but not a niche dimension; Dangles et al. 2009 discuss sensory information use in ecology. The term makes a single heritable sensory variant define a new perceptual niche in an unchanged measured habitat, and so a unit of divergence.

Prior art

von Uexküll 1934; Hutchinson 1957; Dangles et al. 2009; Stevens 2013.

Test and evidence

Measure discrimination profiles of two variants in one habitat and compare the cue dimensions each acts on.

Falsifier

Variants that differ at a receptor locus show identical behavioural discrimination profiles over the habitat’s measured cue dimensions.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
