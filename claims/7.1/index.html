7.1 The organism–field coupling is the interactor

Claim

Within the scientific description, the interactor is the organism coupled to its perceived and enacted field, and the replicator is the heritable receiver architecture.

Hull (1980) separates replicators, which pass on their structure, from interactors, which meet their surroundings as cohesive wholes so that replication becomes differential. One change in receiver architecture, such as temporal resolution or flicker-fusion frequency, re-sorts the field features an organism can register and act on together. Selection therefore meets the organism–field coupling as one cohesive unit. The field is the organism’s field of access and action, measured under the declared protocol; the claim concerns that description only.

What is new

Hull (1980) and Dawkins (1982) name the interactor as the organism or its extended phenotype; Odling-Smee et al. (2003) add ecological inheritance; von Uexküll (1909) describes the Umwelt without a selection role; Lloyd (1988) separates the questions a unit of selection answers. This claim places the receiver-indexed field inside the interactor. It justifies that placement by whole-field re-sorting from one receiver change, and assigns replication to receiver architecture, not to any environment.

Prior art

Hull 1980; Dawkins 1982; Lloyd 1988/2017; Odling-Smee, Laland & Feldman 2003; von Uexküll 1909.

Test and evidence

Compare fitness models in which selection acts on receiver traits one by one with models in which it acts on the coupled organism–field description. The coupled descriptions available are the EFS body-world partition and its coupled repartitioning, the effective phenotype and receiver-relative trait reorganisation, and partition strength q with its selection gradient. The claim is supported where the coupled description predicts fitness variance beyond trait-by-trait models.

Counts against

Fitness is predicted as well by independent, trait-by-trait receiver parameters as by the coupled organism–field partition, so the coupling adds no interactor-level cohesion.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
