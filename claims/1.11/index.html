1.11 Gated decomposition of receiver participation

Claim

A claim that a receiver change took part in an evolutionary process decomposes into five separately testable links, plus reproductive isolation for speciation, each tested against its simplest alternative. The links are receiver variation, discrimination difference, effect on action, ecological outcome and heritable component.

Each link has a named alternative: general sensitivity change, learning, motivation or motor differences. A result that passes every gate except heritability stands as a functional claim, not an evolutionary one. Finding a threshold does not refute a partition, because a threshold usually implements a boundary.

What is new

Levis & Pfennig 2016 give ordered criteria for plasticity-first evolution; Endler 1992 and Cummings & Endler 2018 list sensory-drive components. This adds a gate-by-gate ladder for receiver participation with named failure conditions.

Prior art

Endler 1992; Cummings & Endler 2018; Levis & Pfennig 2016.

Test and evidence

Checklist applied in a book-length treatment in preparation.

Counts against

The receiver claim fails if general sensitivity change explains the difference, if receiver change does not alter generalisation, if categories do not transfer to new stimuli, if the effect vanishes after controls for learning, motivation and motor ability, or if action is unaffected.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
