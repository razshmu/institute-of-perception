1.9 NPH amplification formalism

Claim

Because one sensory feature S enters the perception of every environmental trait (P_i = φ(S)·E_i), a small change in S shifts all perceived traits together and scales the selection gradient on S with the number of traits it touches.

the author’s private archive of the NPH paper formalises NPH with P = Φ(S, E), a multiplicative term across n traits, a fitness function W(P) and a breeder’s-equation response. It derives predictions: perceptual diversification tracks speciation, perceptual innovations precede radiations, and sensory pollution drives evolutionary responses. It specifies a CRISPR test on Drosophila flicker-fusion frequency (1.13).

What is new

Sensory drive states the cross-context coupling verbally but carries no term linking one sensory feature to all perceived traits. What is offered here is the formal amplification term, not the coupling it formalises. Fuller, Houle & Travis 2005 already propose testing the resulting cross-context correlated response by reciprocal selection, so the experimental consequence is not new either.

Prior art

Endler 1992; Seehausen et al. 2008; von Uexküll; Fuller, Houle & Travis 2005, The American Naturalist 166(4):437–446, doi 10.1086/444443 (added); Cummings & Endler 2018 (added).

Test and evidence

Drosophila CFF edit (1.13); comparative tests of the three predictions.

Counts against

A genetic change in one sensory feature (for example CFF) produces behavioural or fitness change no larger than a comparable single morphological change.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
