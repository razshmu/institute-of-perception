11.5 Access-path attribution grammar

Claim

A measured difference is attributed to a target only after its access path is specified and compared with alternative paths; attribution is licensed where the target’s sensitivity direction lies outside the span of the path’s sensitivity directions, and what survives path substitution is credited to the target.

The grammar runs along the whole receiver chain: receptor, organism, instrument and community. The span condition is a local identifiability criterion: if a combination of path changes can mimic a target change, the data cannot separate them. The claim passport renders the grammar as a seven-row form: measured quantity, access path, conversions, calibration and traceability, shared failure points, alternative models, and claim scope. Empty rows mark where the claim is weak.

What is new

Robustness analysis (Wimsatt), measurement coherence (Chang, Tal), metrological traceability (VIM) and statistical local identifiability exist separately. The grammar adds one stepwise attribution procedure across biological and technical receivers, a check on the independence of the paths themselves, and a fillable claim-level form.

Prior art

Wimsatt 1981; Chang 2004; Tal 2017; Stegenga & Menon 2017; Ihde 1990; Massimi 2022; Kane 2013; Bogen & Woodward 1988; JCGM 200:2012; Rothenberg 1971.

Test and evidence

Worked applications of the grammar to biological and instrument cases, with protocols, in a book-length treatment in preparation.

Counts against

Across the worked cases, the grammar never classifies a claim differently from current practice and never blocks an inference that was actually made.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
