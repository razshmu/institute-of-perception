11.6 Diagnostic Access Correction Framework (DACF)

Claim

DACF audits a single empirical claim by separating what its access chain stabilized from the target relation the claim asserts, and ends every audit with a written corrected claim: stand, narrow, qualify or refuse.

DACF separates seven objects: access product, operational relation, target relation, admissible artifact route, diagnostic contrast, route profile and corrected claim. An artifact route is admissible only if it is specific, connected to the access chain, able to generate the operational relation or weaken the bridge to the target, relevant to the claim, and not already contained. Each admissible route is profiled by origin, propagation, containment and decisiveness, and receives one of six ordinal, non-compensatory dispositions. The audit ends only when the disposition is written out as the strongest corrected claim the evidence licenses.

What is new

Kane’s argument-based validation and construct-validity theory examine the score-to-interpretation step; ROBINS-I, QUADAS-2, GRADE and COSMIN grade bias by domain. DACF adds the operational-to-target bridge as audit object, a symmetric five-criterion admissibility rule for admitting and dismissing artifact routes, and a mandatory corrected claim in place of a score.

Prior art

Kane 2013; Cronbach & Meehl 1955; Messick 1989; Borsboom et al. 2004; Bogen & Woodward 1988; Toulmin 1958; Wimsatt 1981; Woodward 2006; ROBINS-I (Sterne et al. 2016); QUADAS-2 (Whiting et al. 2011); GRADE (Guyatt et al. 2008); COSMIN (Mokkink et al. 2010); JCGM 2008, 2012; Jüni et al. 1999.

Test and evidence

Worked XMRV reconstruction (11.9). Proposed study: reviewers write an ordinary review, then a DACF decomposition, of 20–30 historically resolved claims, including positive and mixed cases; primary outcome is convergence of corrected claims across reviewers and with the historical resolution.

Counts against

In the reviewer study, DACF corrected claims converge no better, and match historical resolutions no better, than ordinary prose reviews of the same cases.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
