1.6 Primate trichromacy as candidate receiver-first loop

Claim

Primate trichromacy is a candidate receiver-first loop: an L/M opsin change improved red-green discrimination, shifted fruit choice, altered seed dispersal, and so changed selection on fruit colour and on colour vision.

The standard account reads trichromacy as adaptation to pre-existing fruit or leaf colour. This hypothesis adds the return path through dispersal and plant colour evolution. Each link is testable in polymorphic platyrrhines.

What is new

Sumner & Mollon 2000, Regan et al. 2001 and Dominy & Lucas 2001 explain trichromacy by fruit or young-leaf detection; Onstein et al. 2020 relate palm fruit colour to frugivore vision. This adds the closed receiver-initiated loop with feedback on plant traits.

Prior art

Sumner & Mollon 2000; Regan et al. 2001; Dominy & Lucas 2001; Melin et al. 2017; Onstein et al. 2020.

Test and evidence

Within-population comparison of foraging and seed deposition by opsin genotype in capuchins and spider monkeys (Melin et al. 2017).

Falsifier

Within polymorphic platyrrhine groups, trichromats and dichromats do not differ in choice of red-shifted fruit or in seed deposition; or fruit colour shows no association with primate vision after controlling for bird dispersers.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
