12.6 Alignment clusters are built, not sampled

Claim

Alignment clusters between artificial networks and brains are products of niche construction. People built the images, categories and benchmarks the systems train on, and these pass across model generations by ecological inheritance. Measuring constraints where systems are found conditions on a variable those systems helped produce.

Because the constraint space is endogenous, broader sampling of existing systems inherits the selection. A feedback account predicts that clusters tighten across model generations and show hysteresis; a static clustering account has no term for either. A feedback claim needs three demonstrated links, and current alignment studies establish at most one.

What is new

Bosch et al. 2026 explain alignment by shared ecological constraints taken as given; Dalla Riva 2026 notes that LLM outputs make the world variable non-exogenous. This claim transfers niche-construction diagnostics (choice vs conformance vs construction, collider bias, the three-link feedback burden) to machine-learning alignment and adds tightening and hysteresis as distinguishing predictions.

Prior art

Odling-Smee et al. 2003; Trappes et al. 2022; Bosch et al. 2026; Gröger et al. 2026; Dalla Riva 2026.

Test and evidence

Proposal to Trends in Cognitive Sciences (reply to Bosch et al.); prediction stated, not tested.

Counts against

Alignment clusters neither tighten nor show hysteresis across successive model generations trained on increasingly model-influenced data.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
