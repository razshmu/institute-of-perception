Time · Thesis 15 · Central thesis

15.1 Complete self-representation has two bottlenecks: acquisition and computation

Claim

Complete self-representation requires two separate achievements, acquiring and certifying a complete specification of initial state and dynamics, and computing the target state from it; Wolpert’s lemma establishes the second and FGL constrain the first.

Wolpert’s recursion-theorem construction builds the self-simulating program from the encoded evolution function and interval, and the environmental initial state w_0 enters as input. Fields, Glazebrook and Levin restrict endogenous acquisition and validation of a complete model. The two results therefore address different steps. The operative divide is program construction versus state acquisition versus state computation.

What is new

15.9 reconciled the two papers through generic versus special systems, inductive versus constructed models, and fixed versus delay-grown capacity. This account replaces the “inductive versus analytic” axis, locates the unproven step (an endogenous procedure for obtaining w_0 and g) and yields a four-question audit of input provenance.

Prior art

Breuer 1995; Wolpert 2008; Rummens & Cuypers 2010; Kleene 1952; Moore 1956; Fields, Glazebrook & Levin 2024; Wolpert 2025.

Test and evidence

Formal comparison of the stated inputs of each construction.

Falsifier

A construction inside Wolpert’s framework that endogenously acquires and certifies the complete w_0 required for self-simulation, with a demonstrated correspondence to the completeness FGL exclude.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
