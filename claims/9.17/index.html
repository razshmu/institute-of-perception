9.17 Receiver-envelope sensitivity of footprint partitions

Claim

Across 1,949 DinoTracker footprint silhouettes, reducing spatial access through a graded series of receiver envelopes changes the number of connected regions monotonically, from 2.3% of silhouettes under a Homo-Pan LCA envelope to 24.6% under a compound-eye comparator.

Seven representations of each unchanged 100x100 silhouette were compared: the source, five progressively lower-resolution camera-eye envelopes, and a coarse compound-eye mosaic. Median IoU with the source fell from 0.999 to 0.803, and the partition-change rate rose from 2.3% to 24.6%. Thin, fragmented and incompletely preserved tracks were the least stable. Categorical properties of a fossil outline, including its unit count, are therefore not invariant to receiver resolution.

What is new

Ichnology treats outline segmentation as a property of the track (Lallensack 2019; Hartmann et al. 2026 DinoTracker). This result applies a phylogenetically ordered series of receiver envelopes to the full DinoTracker set and reports a dose-response of individuation change. Register / cover filter and illumination on 37 photogrammetric surfaces, not resolution envelopes on silhouettes.

Prior art

Hartmann et al. 2026 (DinoTracker); Lallensack 2019; Falkingham et al. 2018 documentation standard; Caves, Nowicki & Johnsen 2019 (acuity and signal appearance)

Test and evidence

Executed analysis on all 1,949 binary silhouettes; seven envelopes; partition change = change in number of connected components >20 px. Next test: raw photographs and 3D surfaces of known-maker tracks classified blind by experts.

Falsifier

A partition-change rate that is flat or non-monotone across the envelope series, or that vanishes when the same envelopes are applied to raw surfaces of known-maker tracks.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
