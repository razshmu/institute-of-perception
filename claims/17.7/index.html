17.7 Four layers of standpoint dependence ordered by correctability

Claim

Standpoint dependence in measurement has four layers ordered by correctability: unit choice, instrument bias, species-specific accessibility and conceptual framing; calibration removes the second, reduces the third and leaves the fourth untouched.

Units convert without loss, but they are chosen inside the chooser’s working range: the second sits near the heartbeat, the metre near the stride. Instrument bias is corrected by metrology, where calibration relates indications to reference values with a stated uncertainty (JCGM 200:2012). Instruments reduce species-specific accessibility and never remove it. Conceptual framing, the choice of which quantities to measure, precedes calibration and lies outside its reach.

What is new

Tal (2017) shows calibration is model-based; Chang (2004) and Kuhn show that measurement depends on framing; structural realism and Massimi separate a shared relational core from local presentation. This claim adds an explicit correctability ordering across four layers, blocking both ‘everything is receiver-dependent’ and ‘calibration solves everything’, and ties the layers to two claim-passport rows.

Prior art

JCGM 200:2012 (VIM); Tal 2017; Chang 2004; Kuhn 1970; Hacking 1983; Worrall 1989; Massimi 2022.

Test and evidence

Conceptual analysis with metrological examples. Operationalised by the passport rows ‘traceability’ (the achievement) and ‘shared failure points’ (the limit).

Falsifier

Calibration alone changes which quantity a field measures. That would place framing inside calibration’s reach and break the ordering.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
