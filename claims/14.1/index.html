14.1 Declared normalization frame for cross-system comparison

Claim

Any comparison between systems requires a declared normalization frame; when none is declared, the frame defaults silently to the observer’s own system, as the Earth-orbit year does for cosmic history.

A year is set by one planet’s orbit around one star, so cosmic history is reported in units of a local accident. The unit converts losslessly, yet it controls what looks significant: in fractions of cosmic age the Earth forms near 0.67 and humans appear at 0.9999. Astrophysics already measures each gravitating system in its internal dynamical time, about (Gρ)^-1/2. The same discipline governs biological comparison across receivers.

What is new

Dynamical-time normalization is standard in stellar and galactic dynamics (Binney & Tremaine 2008). The claim generalizes it into a rule for all cross-system comparison, names the undeclared default as the observer’s own system, and treats biological and cosmological cases as one discipline.

Prior art

Binney & Tremaine 2008; Lindstedt & Calder 1981; Schmidt-Nielsen 1984.

Test and evidence

Structural regularities across star clusters, galaxies and galaxy clusters should align better in internal dynamical times than in years (14.7, 14.14).

Counts against

Across gravitating systems spanning several decades in mean density, relaxation-state indicators align no better in dynamical times than in years.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
