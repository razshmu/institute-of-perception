3.15 The ruler problem

Claim

A manual measurement is a perceived target/standard ratio, so its log bias is ρ_target − ρ_standard: category remapping cancels when target and standard share a calibration class and yields a bias factor exp(ρ_target − ρ_standard) when they do not.

At ρ = 0.139 the cross-category bias is +14.9% (predator target, neutral ruler) and −13.0% (neutral target, predator ruler); at ρ = 0.30 it is +35.0% and −25.9%. The bias is invisible to an observer whose standards all share one calibration class. It becomes visible when the same target is measured with standards from two classes, or across observers with different ρ. Remapping predicts category-specific bias; a global gain predicts equal bias in same- and cross-category conditions.

What is new

Measurement theory treats calibration as a property of instruments and procedures. This model shows how an evolved perceptual calibration enters a manual measurement, and derives a category-specific signature that separates remapping from gain.

Prior art

Tal 2017 (calibration); Ernst & Banks 2002.

Test and evidence

Simulation study (companion simulation package; AMP supplement S4.7); the values follow analytically from exp(±ρ).

Falsifier

If measurement bias is equal in same-category and cross-category conditions, or a global-gain model fits cross-category data as well as remapping, the ruler model fails for that observer.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
