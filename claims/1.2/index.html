1.2 Receiver-first evolution (RFE)

Claim

A heritable change in receiver sensory state, such as an opsin or odorant-receptor variant, starts divergence in mate choice and habitat preference while the focal cue field is held constant.

RFE reverses the usual order of sensory drive: the receiver varies first, and behaviour diverges before any change in signals or cue field. The claim is scoped to conditions where the focal cue dimensions stay within a stated tolerance band (2.8). How often the route operates in nature is a separate frequency question (1.8).

What is new

Sensory drive (Endler 1992) starts from the environment; sensory exploitation (Ryan 1990; Endler & Basolo 1998) has pre-existing receiver bias shaping signals, not habitat divergence. RFE makes receiver variation the initiating step for ecological divergence, not only for signal design.

Prior art

Endler 1992; Ryan 1990; Endler & Basolo 1998; Ryan & Cummings 2013; Servedio et al. 2011; Tait et al. 2021.

Test and evidence

Gene-edit or line-cross design (1.13) with the flat-E assay (2.8) and route-priority analysis (2.7).

Falsifier

Lines differing only in the edited receiver gene show no divergence in mate choice or habitat preference under matched cue fields, or behaviour diverges only after cue-field divergence.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
