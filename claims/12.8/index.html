12.8 Access profile with decision-theoretic access

Claim

Access to a target contrast is the improvement in expected decision performance over a no-observation baseline; access profiles sharing a target are ordered by Blackwell garbling, and profiles with different estimands are comparable only through a justified bridge.

An access profile records target-state space, output space, stochastic channel, estimand, scale, individuation rule, calibration, bridge into a shared claim space, uncertainty and dependency membership. In the binary equal-prior case the discrimination advantage equals half the total-variation distance between the two output distributions. Blackwell dominance orders profiles on a shared state space as a preorder. Without a shared estimand, comparison is unlicensed until a bridge model is supplied.

What is new

Blackwell (1953) orders statistical experiments; perspectival realism and robustness analysis discuss instrument mediation. The profile adds individuation rule, scale, bridge and dependency membership to the channel, and shows that extension of access is not monotone.

Prior art

Blackwell 1953; Giere 2006; Wimsatt 1981; Tal 2017.

Test and evidence

Applied to cichlid visual-signal evidence (spectra, visual models, behaviour, mating) and a transcriptomics integration case.

Falsifier

In a benchmark, two routes that the profile marks as Blackwell-ordered on a shared target yield decision performance ordered opposite to the garbling relation.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
