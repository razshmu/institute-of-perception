8.4 Labelling-plus-discrimination rule separates boundary shifts from criterion shifts

Claim

A boundary shift is diagnosed only when the discrimination peak moves with the labelling boundary, and a preregistered confidence-bound rule classified no true boundary shift as a criterion shift in 14,400 simulated replicates.

The rule tests labelling and peak shifts with Welch t-tests and calls a criterion shift only when the upper confidence bound on the peak shift falls below half the labelling shift. Across 24 boundary-shift conditions of 600 replicates each, the criterion-shift rate was 0 in every condition. When power was short, the rule returned ‘inconclusive’ instead of a wrong category. Discrimination trials set power: tripling them raised correct classification by up to 0.45.

What is new

Liberman et al. (1957) define categorical perception by a discrimination peak at the labelling boundary; Caves et al. (2018) separate sensitivity from categorisation. New: a preregistered decision rule with a quantified error profile against named competitors (criterion shift, baseline sensitivity, peak-amplitude sensitivity, no difference), plus a structure card for learning, attention and action-policy alternatives.

Prior art

Liberman et al. 1957; Macmillan & Creelman 2005; Caves et al. 2018; Green, Brandley & Nowicki 2020; Lachlan & Nowicki 2015; McMurray 2022.

Test and evidence

Simulation S4 (24 boundary-shift conditions x 600 = 14,400; criterion rate 0.0 in all); receiver-boundary article, section 5c.

Falsifier

Empirical data in which a single decision-criterion parameter reproduces both the labelling shift and a moved discrimination peak; or a simulation variant (for example a non-Gaussian peak) in which the rule misclassifies boundary shifts as criterion shifts above 5%.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
