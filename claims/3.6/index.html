3.6 Acceleration bias depends on how velocity change is reconstructed

Claim

Under category-linked log-gains on perceptual primitives, perceived-acceleration bias is rho_dv - rho_t if velocity change is a primitive, rho_x - 2rho_t if derived from displacement over time, and a cue-geometry function if inferred from looming, so matched space-time gains cancel velocity bias but not derived acceleration bias.

Velocity, momentum, kinetic energy and time-to-contact have fixed log-bias expressions, but acceleration and force do not until the organism’s route to delta-v is specified. Three cases are separated: velocity-change primitive, derived velocity-change, and optical/looming proxy. Force inherits the chosen case, B(F) = rho_m + B(a). The case split changes predictions for matched spatial and temporal gains and adds a diagnostic signature against criterion shift.

What is new

The prior art gives cancellation in velocity, amplification in kinetic energy and isolation in time-to-contact. This adds the three-case acceleration/force table and shows that B(a) = rho_x - rho_t holds only when rho_dv equals rho_x.

Prior art

Size-weight illusion literature (Buckingham & Goodale 2013); tau/looming theory (Lee 1976); register.

Test and evidence

Formal derivation (Simulation 4 tables). Test: measure separate space, time, heaviness and acceleration judgments for one category, then compare fits of the three delta-v models.

Falsifier

Acceleration judgments under matched space-time gains show no residual bias of -rho_t, and no delta-v model fits better than the velocity-based expression.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
