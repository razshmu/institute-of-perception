2.4 Cue-mediated magic-trait reporting standard

Claim

Every empirical magic-trait claim reports four elements: the phenotypic coupling route on the E-Os-Oi map, the architecture maintaining the association (pleiotropy or linkage), the evidence level (covariance, functional duality, causal manipulation, resolved architecture) and the coupling effect size.

The standard classifies cases as strict magic trait, pleiotropic architecture, pseudomagic complex, mixed route or association-only candidate. Route and architecture are reported separately because the same route can rest on pleiotropy or on linkage disequilibrium. The 23 June draft states the effect-size target as ΔM = E[M | do(T = t1)] − E[M | do(T = t2)] for direct routes. The parent claim is 1.7.

What is new

Servedio et al. 2011 define magic traits; Maan & Seehausen 2012 separate magic cues from magic preferences; Servedio & Bürger 2020 treat pseudomagic traits. This adds a four-field reporting standard with a route map and a do-operator estimand.

Prior art

Servedio et al. 2011; Maan & Seehausen 2012; Servedio & Bürger 2020; Kopp et al. 2018.

Test and evidence

Applied to worked examples (Heliconius, Lake Victoria cichlids, Rhagoletis) in the Perspective drafts.

Counts against

Applying the standard to published cases changes none of their classifications relative to the existing magic/pseudomagic categories.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
