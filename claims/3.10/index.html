3.10 Multi-anchor discriminator: remapping versus criterion shift

Claim

Metric remapping predicts a log-scale PSE shift that is constant across anchors, while an additive criterion shift predicts a log-scale shift ln((s+α)/s) that falls with the standard s; the two coincide at a single anchor.

Under remapping the category-P test is scaled by exp(ρ_P), so its log PSE shift is ρ_P at every standard. At ρ_P = 0.139 and α_P = 0.15 the shifts are 0.139 versus 0.172, 0.140 and 0.113 at s = 0.8, 1.0 and 1.25. The design fits ρ_c in a shared psychometric model against criterion, gain, lapse, arousal, learning, stimulus-energy and motor-policy terms.

What is new

Psychometric model comparison and the perception-versus-response-bias debate (García-Pérez & Alcalá-Quintana 2013; Witt et al. 2015) show that single-point shifts are ambiguous. This measure uses the anchor-dependence of the log shift as the identifying signature and makes ρ_c a fitted parameter.

Prior art

Prins & Kingdom 2018; Wichmann & Hill 2001; García-Pérez & Alcalá-Quintana 2013; Witt et al. 2015.

Test and evidence

A validation report reproduces the three-anchor values above.

Counts against

Across ≥3 anchors spanning a preregistered range, a fitted log shift that follows ln((s+α)/s), or remapping failing to beat additive, gain and lapse models by the preregistered ΔAIC/ELPD margin, rejects remapping for that dataset.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
