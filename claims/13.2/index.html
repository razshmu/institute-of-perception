13.2 Error inheritance: downstream correctness cannot repair upstream misindividuation

Claim

If an early assignment maps two world states that differ in the claim-relevant target to the same labelled data, every downstream stage without independent target evidence yields identical output distributions, so no downstream analysis can separate the correct target from the wrong one.

Let s map records X to labelled data D1 = s(X), followed by transformations f_i. If s(x) = s(x’) for states differing in target assignment, all later outputs coincide in distribution under x and x’. This holds for deterministic maps and for Markov chains of stochastic kernels. A flawless model can therefore estimate the wrong unit with narrow intervals, and only evidence that reopens the target assignment discriminates the routes.

What is new

The data-processing inequality is standard. The claim applies it to target individuation, defines ‘inherited-error cut points’ after which target-distinguishing information is gone, and derives an audit step that locates them. Worked on DeGrave et al. 2021 COVID radiographs.

Prior art

Data-processing inequality (Cover & Thomas); shortcut learning (Geirhos et al. 2020); DeGrave, Janizek & Lee 2021; Stegenga & Menon 2017.

Counts against

The formal core follows from the data-processing inequality. The empirical test fails if, in audited pipelines, located cut points do not predict where external validation fails.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
