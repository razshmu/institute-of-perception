2.10 27-regime factorial intervention grammar

Claim

Holding each of E, Os and Oi high, low or free defines a 3 × 3 × 3 grammar of 27 regimes, of which the twelve two-fixed/one-free regimes are the clean local route tests.

A contrast is a route test only when the compared cells share the same free outcome and background and differ in one manipulated factor. Oi-free modules estimate E→Oi, Os→Oi and E×Os; Os-free modules estimate E→Os and Oi→Os carryover; E-free modules estimate Oi→E and test that apparent Os→E effects vanish when Oi is clamped. All-free regimes are the open benchmark, one-fixed regimes give total effects, and all-fixed regimes are clamp-leak and drift controls.

What is new

Do-calculus clamping (Pearl), factorial and difference-in-difference designs, and feedback-on/off designs exist separately. The grammar derives the practical diagnostics (2.8–2.11) and the structural zero (2.4) as cells of one factorial space, specifies which comparisons are interpretable, and assigns roles to the other regimes.

Prior art

Pearl 2009; full-factorial and difference-in-difference designs; Endler 1992; Boughman 2002; Laland et al. (feedback on/off).

Test and evidence

Run the Oi-free, Os-free and E-free modules in one system (host-odour blends × olfactory state × host-choice access) and check route estimates against route-asymmetry comparisons (2.7).

Falsifier

An E-free module with Oi clamped still shows an Os effect on E that survives manipulation checks, or two-fixed/one-free contrasts reverse under difference-in-difference tests at the other level of the fixed factor.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
