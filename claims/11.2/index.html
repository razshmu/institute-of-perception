11.2 Non-automatic claim-status inheritance and residual claims

Claim

Warrant does not pass automatically from a result to a transformed claim. Content reused for a different target, relation or strength needs its warrant re-established, and the audit outputs the strongest residual claim that survives.

The audit unit is the claim transition, not the paper or author. Warrant carries across a transition only along a named invariance: a calibration, a validated transformation or a deduction. Each transition is coded as preserved, narrowed, routed or unsupported. The residual-claim operation finds the first unsupported claim-critical dependency and rewrites the claim to the strongest licensed version; it applies equally to citation drift.

What is new

Bogen & Woodward separate data from phenomena; Kane requires an interpretive argument per score use; Greenberg documents citation distortion. This claim adds the transition as audit unit, the named-invariance rule for inheritance, and a residual claim that keeps the bounded result.

Prior art

Bogen & Woodward 1988; Kane 2013; Stevens 1946; Tal 2017; Greenberg 2009; SciFact (Wadden et al. 2020).

Test and evidence

Worked transitions in Containment Is Not Inheritance and a book-length treatment in preparation; domain applications in 11.6, 11.7, 11.8. A cosmology application names five levels of decreasing warrant: data, map, statistic, parameter, model.

Counts against

In a sample of cited claims, strength-changing transitions without a named invariance hold up under later tests as often as transitions with one.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
