8.1 Category-boundary location is an individual phenotype

Claim

We treat the location of a categorical boundary as an individual reaction-norm phenotype, with repeatability, plasticity, additive-genetic variance and a fitness effect that standard designs can estimate.

A receiver’s boundary b_i is a latent parameter of its psychometric function, re-estimable in the same individual across sessions. Treated as a reaction norm, it has an intercept, a slope on experienced signal distribution, and a persistence. Its evolutionary role then splits into three separate hypotheses: heritable location, changed number or organisation of categories, and a heritable learning rule. Each has its own test, so a null result for heritable location leaves the learning-rule hypothesis open.

What is new

Peniston et al. (2020) showed that the distribution of receiver thresholds shapes signal reliability but held that distribution fixed. Dingemanse et al. (2010) supply the reaction-norm apparatus for behaviour. Nelson & Marler (1989) and Caves et al. (2018) establish categorical boundaries. This claim adds boundary location as a quantitative-genetic phenotype (variance components, a place in G) and the three-way split of the evolutionary hypothesis.

Prior art

Categorical perception (Nelson & Marler 1989; Baugh et al. 2008; Caves et al. 2018; Green et al. 2020); Peniston et al. 2020; Dingemanse et al. 2010; Mendelson et al. 2016.

Test and evidence

Model and designs in the receiver-boundary article (sections 3 and 6); split-brood and half-sib simulations S3, S3b and S5.

Counts against

Repeated measurement shows b_i is not repeatable within individuals; or half-sib or selection-line designs with power at least 0.8 for h2 = 0.1 bound Var_A(b_i) below that value; or male mating success shows no dependence on boundary straddling at matched physical distance (gamma = 0).

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
