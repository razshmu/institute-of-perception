11.11 Claim-audit pilot results

Claim

In a single-coder purposive pilot of 13 claim instances from 11 sensory-ecology papers, six were preserved, five narrowed and two routed out of scope. The narrowings fell at the step from sensory mechanism to behavioural or evolutionary consequence.

The pilot covered cichlid sensory drive, poeciliid receiver bias, Rhagoletis olfaction and anthropogenic sensory pollution. Measurement and attribution claims were preserved, for example Maan et al. 2006 on habitat-matched visual sensitivity. Narrowing fell on claims that moved from that sensitivity to preference, isolation or speciation. The two out-of-scope records required phylogenetic reconstruction.

What is new

Case discussions of sensory drive (Seehausen et al. 2008; Wright et al. 2020) note that the sensitivity-to-preference link is weakly tested. The pilot adds a coded claim-instance record that locates each narrowing at that transition.

Prior art

Maan et al. 2006; Wright et al. 2020; Seehausen et al. 1997, 2008; Carleton et al. 2005; Senzaki et al. 2020; Basolo 1990, 1996.

Test and evidence

Supplementary coding trail in the Animal Behaviour SI (pass13).

Falsifier

Independent recoding of the 13 records places the narrowings at other transitions, for example at cue-to-stimulus measurement.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
