Time · Thesis 14 · Testable hypothesis

14.6 Cross-species alignment in Θ

Claim

Cross-species sensory temporal-resolution data align better in Θ than in seconds when τ_sens is set by 1/CFF or by an allometric metabolic mapping fitted on separate species.

We test whether a physiological τ reduces cross-species dispersion out of sample. CFF sets τ for sensory resolution tasks. Metabolic and body-size mappings set τ where no CFF value exists. Support requires that a preregistered, restricted τ family beat seconds on held-out species; success obtained only through per-species freedom counts as rejection.

What is new

Healy et al. 2013 show that CFF scales with body mass and metabolic rate. The claim adds an out-of-sample normalisation test with a pre-specified τ and an explicit anti-overfitting rejection rule.

Prior art

Healy et al. 2013; Lindstedt & Calder 1981; Raz & Breitkopf 2024.

Test and evidence

Held-out species prediction of temporal-resolution measures, comparing dispersion in seconds against dispersion in Θ.

Falsifier

Normalisation fails to reduce dispersion beyond a single global rescaling, the gain vanishes on held-out species, or restricted τ models succeed only by becoming per-species fits.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
