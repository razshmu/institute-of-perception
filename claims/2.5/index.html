2.5 Route-priority test

Claim

Receiver-led and sorting-led ordering are distinguished by whether prior receiver state Os(t) or a non-circular exposure proxy better predicts later Oi(t+1) out of sample under matched E, with the causal graph stated before model comparison.

Receiver-led support requires Os(t) to improve held-out prediction of Oi(t+1) after prior Oi and measured E_exp are included. The symmetric criterion supports an experience-led route. Stating the graph in advance prevents post hoc route selection.

What is new

Granger causality (Granger 1969) and convergent cross mapping (Sugihara et al. 2012) test predictive ordering in time series. This adds matched-E conditions and a non-circular exposure proxy (2.15), so the comparison bears on receiver-first versus sorting.

Prior art

Granger 1969; Sugihara et al. 2012; Deyle et al. 2016; Vehtari, Gelman & Gabry 2017.

Test and evidence

In simulation, route asymmetry raised classification from 64% to 77% (2.14).

Counts against

Os(t) adds no out-of-sample prediction of Oi(t+1) beyond prior Oi and E_exp in a system claimed as receiver-led.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.

Thesis 2: Chapter 3. Telling an environmental cause from a sensory one
