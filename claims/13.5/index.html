13.5 Perceptual pointability predicts definitional disputes in science

Claim

Scientific disputes over what counts as one instance concentrate on concepts whose referents human perception does not deliver as bounded, cohesive and persisting objects and that no instrument has converted into such objects.

Blind raters score scientific terms, mixed among everyday items, on Spelke’s object criteria (boundary, cohesion, persistence). Low-scoring concepts (species, niche, ecosystem, holobiont) are predicted to carry higher rates of definitional literature than high-scoring ones (bacterium, ribosome, neuron). A second prediction is that an instrument that makes a referent pointable closes the corresponding dispute. The design uses random MeSH and Wikipedia sampling frames, a factorial frame that breaks the collectivity confound, and sealed predictions.

What is new

Individuation literatures (Spelke 1990; Tal 2019; McConwell 2024) study how perception or practice individuates, but none predicts where scientific definitional disputes cluster from a pre-scientific perceptual score. This turns receiver-relative individuation into a bibliometric test.

Prior art

Spelke 1990; Tal 2019 (Individuating Quantities); McConwell 2024; species-concept pluralism (Kitcher 1984; Dupre 1993); McAllister 2026

Test and evidence

Pre-registered, sealed protocols; rater ICC 0.898 and 0.972. All quantitative outcomes of that run were voided because Europe PMC TITLE queries do not phrase-match. A valid outcome needs single-word markers (individuation, delimitation) or recorded identity databases (Gaia, ComCat).

Falsifier

With a validated dependent variable, randomly sampled concepts show no negative association between pointability score and definitional-dispute rate. Or disputes persist unchanged after an instrument makes a referent bounded and trackable.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
