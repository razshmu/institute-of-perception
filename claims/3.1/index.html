3.1 Spatial AMP: a heritable baseline calibration term

Claim

Perceived extent of an ecologically routed feature class decomposes as d’(C,s) = f0(C)·g(C,s)·d, and the evolutionary claim attaches only to the baseline term f0(C), never to the state term g.

We model perceived extent d’ of a stimulus of physical extent d in feature class C and internal state s as f0(C)·g(C,s)·d. The state term g absorbs arousal, vigilance, attention, task context and learned expectation. The spatial AMP claim is that f0(C) ≠ 1 persists after g is attenuated or modelled out. Selection acts on the circuit-building and tuning rule that rebuilds f0 each generation, not on the percept.

What is new

Threat-size effects (Vasey et al. 2012) and action-specific perception (Stefanucci & Proffitt 2009) report category-linked size shifts without separating a baseline from a state component. Receiver-bias and sensory-drive work places selection on detection and preference. This claim adds an explicit baseline/state decomposition of perceived extent and restricts the evolutionary claim to the baseline term.

Prior art

Receiver bias and sensory exploitation (Ryan & Cummings 2013); sensory drive (Endler & Basolo 1998); Vasey et al. 2012; Stefanucci & Proffitt 2009; Sperandio & Chouinard 2015.

Test and evidence

Formal statement in the Adaptive Metric Perception (AMP) paper (§4.1, S1.1). Tested by the multi-anchor discriminator under state controls (fed/starved/re-fed, octopamine, locomotor speed) and feature-matched controls.

Falsifier

A category-linked size-equivalence shift that disappears under state attenuation (satiation, octopamine block) or under feature matching, or that criterion, gain or lapse models fit as well across ≥3 anchors, refutes f0(C) ≠ 1 for that lineage.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
