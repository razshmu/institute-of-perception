8.3 No generation count clears non-genetic transmission

Claim

Common-garden rearing for any number of generations does not exclude non-genetic transmission; separating genetic from non-genetic inheritance requires design (reciprocal crosses, cross-fostering, pedigree-based additive variance), and even then shows transmitted variation, not selection.

Methodological reviews recommend at least one full generation in common conditions to reduce maternal effects. Common rearing removes only the environmental differences the design actually equalized. Prenatal effects, egg provisioning, epigenetic marks, microbiome transfer and early learning can persist outside it.

What is new

de Villemereuil et al. (2016) give the one-generation guidance. The addition is that no generation count is sufficient and that design, not time, does the separation.

Prior art

de Villemereuil et al. 2016; Kruuk 2004; Mousseau & Fox 1998; Bonduriansky & Day 2009 (added).

Test and evidence

Worked in a book-length treatment in preparation.

Counts against

A demonstration that a fixed number of common-garden generations removes all listed non-genetic channels in a given system. That would restrict the claim to other systems.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
