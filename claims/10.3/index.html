10.3 Brake-and-accelerator tempo hypothesis

Claim

A stable receiver-calibration regime buffers the effective environment and yields bounded fluctuation, while rare calibration-regime shifts move many selective optima at once; multi-trait bursts therefore coincide with documented sensory-regime transitions more often than under independence.

Stasis and punctuated release both come from one low-dimensional component. The Bogert effect supplies the brake; RCS adds the accelerator and predicts that release events are rare and coordinated across traits. The test (P5) uses sister-clade contrasts on existing comparative data: cichlid photic regimes, cave lineages and deep-sea transitions.

What is new

Behavioral inertia (Huey et al. 2003) explains buffering; displaced-optimum models (Estes & Arnold 2007; Uyeda et al. 2011) fit bursts without naming what displaces optima. This hypothesis names a candidate displacer and a timing prediction.

Prior art

Huey, Hertz & Kingsolver 2003; Estes & Arnold 2007; Gingerich 1983; Uyeda et al. 2011; Wyles et al. 1983.

Test and evidence

RCS manuscript, §5 and P5.

Falsifier

Burst timing is statistically independent of sensory-system transitions in sister-clade contrasts.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
