1.8 Receiver-first as a frequency hypothesis

Claim

How often receiver-first routes operate is an empirical frequency question, decided system by system by four abandonment conditions. Receiver-first status is abandoned when prior receiver state fails to predict behaviour under matched conditions, when place history predicts behaviour better, when masking and rescue change nothing, or when environmental changes do not persist and return.

The thesis is a claim about frequency, not possibility. For each candidate system, each of four named results removes receiver-first status. The primate seed-dispersal chain (1.5) is the worked example.

What is new

Endler 1992, Ryan & Cummings 2013 and Odling-Smee et al. 2003 describe receiver bias and niche construction without abandonment conditions. Levis & Pfennig 2016 give such criteria for plasticity-first evolution. This adds per-system abandonment conditions for receiver-first routes.

Prior art

Endler 1992; Endler & Basolo 1998; Ryan & Cummings 2013; Odling-Smee et al. 2003; Levis & Pfennig 2016.

Test and evidence

The four conditions map onto 2.7, 2.11, 2.9 and 2.10.

Counts against

One condition met in a system removes receiver-first status for that system; across systems, the route is rare if most tested cases meet one.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
