5.9 Effective phenotype

Claim

The effective phenotype is the realized organization, at a declared level and time window, of the units, boundaries, properties and relations that a receiver-body-environment system makes functionally available, split into available and recruited parts.

“Effective” means realized, not adaptive. The effective phenotype is the phenotype that selection meets at the interactor level (Thesis 7).

What is new

Extends interaction-phenotype and extended-phenotype ideas to the organization of units and relations; the term was not found in the searched literature.

Prior art

Dawkins 1982; Moore et al. 1997; Reichert & Caves 2026; Diaz 2025.

Test and evidence

Model comparison in 5.5.

Counts against

A partition model fails to explain generalization data that fixed-trait and sampling-only models cannot.

An outcome that weakens or narrows the claim without contradicting it.
