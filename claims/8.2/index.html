8.2 Calibration shields boundary location from selection

Claim

In the reaction norm b_ij = (1-w_i)g_i + w_i m_ij + p_i + e_ij, selection moves the mean boundary by (1-w)^2 sigma2_g beta, and a heritable calibration weight w lets selection act on the learning rule whenever m differs from mean g.

The expressed boundary mixes an intrinsic location g with the antimode m of the experienced signal distribution, weighted by w. With w fixed, the response to linear selection beta is (1-w)^2 sigma2_g beta, so calibration shields location quadratically. With genetic variance in w, a first-order expansion adds (m-g_bar)^2 sigma2_w beta and a covariance term. Because w lies in [0,1], evolution through the rule moves the expressed boundary only within the interval between g_bar and m.

What is new

The quadratic shielding is the known plasticity result (Via & Lande 1985; Price, Qvarnstrom & Irwin 2003; Lande 2009), and the author states this. New: the separation of intrinsic boundary location from a heritable learning rule, the bound that rule-driven evolution cannot carry the boundary beyond m, and the split-brood and half-sib signatures that follow (Table 1).

Prior art

Via & Lande 1985; Price, Qvarnstrom & Irwin 2003; Lande 2009; Dingemanse et al. 2010; Peniston et al. 2020.

Test and evidence

Derivation in the receiver-boundary article (sections 3c-3d); an individual-based simulation reproduces eq. 3 (figure 1b).

Falsifier

A selection-line experiment on a categorical boundary in which the realized response departs from (1-w)^2 sigma2_g beta given independently estimated w and sigma2_g; or a boundary that evolves beyond m under selection on the learning rule alone.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
