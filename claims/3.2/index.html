3.2 Adaptive Metric Perception: selection on equivalence structure

Claim

Selection under asymmetric ecological costs tunes the equivalence structure of perception: which physically different stimuli a lineage treats as equal, larger, nearer or faster, not only what enters a channel.

AMP defines a perceptual metric as an organism-specific equivalence structure within a declared measurement frame. A fitness functional W[ρ(z)] = ∫p(z)U(z,ρ(z))dz − η∫C(ρ(z))dz places a calibration field ρ(z) over ecological feature space. Category-linked spatial tuning is the sparse limit ρ(z) → ρ_C. AMP predicts lineage-specific metric shifts aligned with each lineage’s ecological cost asymmetries.

What is new

Sensory drive, receiver bias, efficient coding and Bayesian natural-selection models show that selection shapes detection, salience, priors and coding. AMP moves the target to the equivalence relations inside a channel and states this as a model-comparison hypothesis with named nuisance rivals.

Prior art

Endler & Basolo 1998; Seehausen et al. 2008; Ryan & Cummings 2013; Simoncelli & Olshausen 2001; Geisler & Diehl 2002, 2003; Stefanucci & Proffitt 2009; Haselton & Buss 2000.

Test and evidence

Framework in the AMP paper §4.1 and S1.3.1; spatial case simulated; comparative programme in the AMP taxon table.

Falsifier

A proposed case fails if nuisance models absorb the effect, feature matching removes it, it fails to generalize across predicted anchors, or it changes response strength without changing the inferred equivalence function. At programme level, independent lineages with opposite cost asymmetries showing same-direction metric shifts count against AMP.

An outcome that contradicts the claim as stated.
