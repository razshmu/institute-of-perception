Young Minds

Real research, earlier than usual.

A research track led by Shmuel Raz for selected students aged 11–17. Not tutoring and not test preparation: genuine scientific work, supervised from the first question to a poster, a presentation or a manuscript.

How to apply

Mission

Our mission: teach young people to ask what the evidence really shows

Every claim the Institute studies passes through a receiver: an eye, an instrument, a model, a press release. The skill that protects science is the same at every level: finding the step where a claim says more than the evidence did.

We think that skill should be learned early, by doing real research. Young Minds is how the Institute passes its method on.

Where the mission comes from

A researcher who started as a student

  1. High school

    Shmuel Raz began research as a high-school student at Evolution Canyon on Mount Carmel, cataloguing the animals of two facing slopes. He published his first paper at 19.

  2. 20+ years

    Since then he has supervised high-school final research projects and mentored B.Sc. and M.Sc. students, from a first question to analysis and writing.

  3. Students' papers

    Students he mentored became co-authors of peer-reviewed papers, including a study of speciation in spiny mice in PNAS (2014) and a survey of snail biodiversity in the Biological Journal of the Linnean Society (2008).

  4. 2026

    The Institute's own science appeared in Frontiers for Young Minds, where young readers review every article before it is published.

What students receive

  • Weekly scientific supervision with Shmuel Raz, with clear milestones
  • Statistical and analytical support where the project requires it
  • The full pipeline: literature, study design, analysis, poster, presentation and manuscript
  • One primary academic output per student, and a selective submission route where the work justifies it

Research domains

  • Sensory perception
  • Self-reported health and wellbeing
  • Behaviour and environment
  • Digital health attitudes
  • Student self-regulation

The pipeline

From a first question to a finished piece of work

  1. 1Screen fit
  2. 2Choose a domain
  3. 3Frame the question
  4. 4Map the literature
  5. 5Run the method and the analysis
  6. 6Write, present, submit

Integrity

  • Students understand, present and defend their own project
  • Authorship only where the contribution is genuine and documented
  • No publication guarantees
  • AI may support the workflow but is never an author

Research ethics and safeguarding

Projects involving people, health information or sensitive data follow age-appropriate consent, privacy and research-ethics requirements. Students do not conduct clinical diagnosis or treatment research.

How to apply

Admission is selective. Send:

  • A short academic profile or CV
  • Current grade, school and country
  • A note on interests and motivation
  • Any earlier project, competition entry or writing sample

Shortlisted students are invited to a first research session to assess fit, readiness and direction.
